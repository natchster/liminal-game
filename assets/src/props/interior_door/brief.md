# interior_door

The brief (`docs/design/ASSETS.md`, *An asset's dossier*). Its
measurements' and palette's sources are in `references.toml`.

## What it is

The ordinary door of a flat's or a small office's rooms: a hollow-core
moulded door, two hardboard skins pressed with six panels over a
softwood frame and a paper honeycomb (refs 3, 13, 14), 813 by 2032 mm and
35 mm thick (2'8" by 6'8" by 1-3/8"), its panels as the moulders lay them
out (refs 10, 11), primed and painted, hung on butt hinges (ref 5) in a
softwood jamb with a colonial casing round it, 57 by 17 mm (ref 17); a
round knob, 54 mm across and 59 mm out, on its rose, 914 mm up in a 54 mm
bore at a 60 mm backset (refs 2, 15, 16). Painted white gone yellow
(ref 1), or someone's colour (a dark red, a brown: ref 4), or a moulded
wood-grain brown; grimy round the knob, chipped at the latch edge.

The rooms keep it two ways. **In its frame**, shut, its jamb and casing
standing against the wall it would open onto: a door to nowhere. **Off its hinges**, its knob gone and the bore empty, shoved into
a doorway in a barricade with a dresser and nailed there.

## How it came here

The pillars: "real doorways, real materials, then one thing wrong (a
doorway to nowhere, a ceiling that steps for no reason)"
(`docs/design/PILLARS.md` 2); and the canon: "Office architecture with no
office in it: doorways to nowhere" (`docs/design/LORE.md`, *Premise*);
"**A door to nowhere**" (*Places*): "behind it is the wall", and why it is
there is never said. And "**Barricades.** ... what the place had shoved
into it ... a home's mattresses, a door off its hinges and a dresser ...
Somebody built them; who, and against what, is never said" (*Places*).

## How it fits

`PILLARS.md` 2: one thing wrong in a real place: a real door at its real
size, in its frame, with its knob, shut against a wall. It must
never say what is behind it, nor be a way: no light under it, no sound, no
hand that opens it. Go round its wall and it is a wall, as the windows'
are (*The windows*): the same wrong, kept on purpose.

## What a hand does

Nothing. It is shut and stays shut: nothing in the canon has a hand try
it. Off its hinges in a barricade, it is part of what stops a body and a
look.

## Where it would be found

On a wall of a place that had rooms with doors: someone's rooms, an
office wing, a parlour, a storage hall, a maintenance run; rarely (on
one floor in about twenty-five), standing on the floor against the wall,
its frame round it. Never in washrooms, a pool hall, a shrine or the
Showroom, whose doors are not a flat's; never on the Lobby ("rooms and
nothing else"), nor on a floor with rules of its own (on the Loop's, a
second plain shut door would stand for its way in). In a barricade, in
the doorways of someone's rooms or a parlour, with the house's other
things, by the barricades' own rules (never on a floor with rules of its
own, one at most on the Lobby). Against a wall as wide as its casing, the
floor clear at both its edges: not on the face of a narrow box, not
squeezed into a corner (the playtest), which the game does not yet check
(`docs/plan/MILESTONES.md` 26).
