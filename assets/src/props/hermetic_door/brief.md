# hermetic_door

The brief (`docs/design/ASSETS.md`, *An asset's dossier*). Its
measurements' and palette's sources are in `references.toml`.

## What it is

A civil-defence hermetic door (гермодверь) of the kind fitted to the
shelters of Soviet panel blocks, the size of a ДУ-IV-2 (an opening 1200 by
2000 mm, its leaf 1266 by 2066 lapping the frame 33 mm a side, its frame
band 108 mm: ref 12): a steel leaf, a plate welded over a grid of channel
ribs (ref 4), here 1240 by 2060 mm and 90 mm thick, on two heavy hinges on
its axis (refs 4, 12), in a steel frame welded to the concrete round its
opening (ref 13); a hand wheel at its middle, 420 mm across with four
spokes (refs 1, 15), on a spindle through the leaf to the gearbox on its
inner face, whose screw rods drive the wedges at its head and foot into
the frame (ref 12); a rubber seal round the leaf's inner face (refs 4,
13). Enamelled grey-green over red-brown primer, many coats (refs 6, 11,
13), worn through to the primer and the steel at its edges and round the
wheel's hub, rust run down from the hinges and come up at its foot (ref
9); its wheel worn to the bare steel where hands have turned it (many are
painted red, ref 1: here red is the cleaners' mark alone); its number on a
plate beside it.

The Well's are the same door, shut from inside: numbered in the
thousands, some with a red cross the cleaners painted across them, some
taped round their edges. No hatch shows on any of them: one is cut in the
foot of a door only as it answers.

## How it came here

The canon: "**Cells**: rooms cut into the building, each with a hermetic
door, its wheel, its number, a bench, and a lamp in a cage over it"
(`docs/design/LORE.md`, *The Building*), "a sealed door every few metres,
numbered in the thousands, some marked red, some taped round" (the Well),
"a door behind every one shut from inside. People lived here once, and a
few still do, behind doors they never open" (*Premise*). "*The sealed door
and the knock at it are the Samosbor's*" (*The Building*, *Knocking*; the
credit in *Attribution and licensing*): the myth's door, "the only shelter
is a sealed hermetic door (гермодверь), and you never open it before the
siren stops" (`docs/design/MEGASTRUCTURE.md`, *What the research says*;
the myth's rule, not the game's: a cell here can be opened before the
all-clear). The red marks are the cleaners' ("the red marks on doors that
did not answer", *The self-assembly*). Who built the cells nobody says
(*The self-assembly*), nor what is behind a marked door, and nothing here
says it.

## How it fits

`PILLARS.md` 2: real materials: a real shelter door at its real size,
steel, painted and worn by years of hands and damp. Pillar 1: the cell is
the shelter, its door turned shut against the fog. Pillar 5: knocking at
a door the cleaners marked ends the quiet, a choice the player makes. Its
marks say only what marks say; nothing on it says who is behind it or
why it is marked, and nothing on a door shows whether it will answer.

## What a hand does

A cell's, what it always did, from inside (`world::cell_knock`): "[E]
TURN THE WHEEL" to shut it and seal it, "[E] OPEN THE DOOR" to go out;
while something knocks at it in the fog, "[E] KNOCK" to answer, and "[E]
HOLD THE WHEEL" once its wheel turns from outside. It is not opened from
inside while that is there, nor until it has gone, and a door that gave
does not shut again until the all-clear (*Things that live here*, *What
knocks at the cell*). A gallery door's: "[E] KNOCK"; it is sealed from
inside and never opens. Most stay silent; four of a floor's ordinary
doors answer once each: a hatch at the foot opens and a bottle or a
battery is pushed out, or a folded note is slid under it. A door marked
red knocks back in your own rhythm, and the siren follows within seconds;
a taped door never answers; and none answers while the siren sounds or
the fog is up (*Knocking*).

## Where it would be found

On the Building's floors only: in the front of every cell, swung open
into it until it is turned shut; and along the Well's galleries, on the
panel faces by the walks, a door every few metres (about ninety a floor,
and the galleries of every storey above and below with theirs). Never in
a room off a walk ("its door gone"), never on the rooms' floors.
