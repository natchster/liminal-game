# service_run

What runs along the Utility Halls' crown (`docs/plan/FACILITY.md` F5; the
research: `docs/research/facility.md`; where it goes: `world::lining`).

## What it is

The services of a plant's tunnel, nobody's make, laid along the crown of
a straight corridor:

- a ladder cable tray of galvanised steel: two C-channel side rails 92 mm
  tall (a 3 in tray), 305 mm apart, rungs every 305 mm, and eight
  PVC-sheathed cables 12 to 32 mm across lying on them, black;
- a pair of steel pipes, 4 in and 2 in (114 and 60 mm outside), in their
  dull paint, in 20 ft lengths (6.1 m) joined by grooved couplings in
  their orange enamel gone dull, each turning up through the crown at its
  run's ends on a long-radius elbow (its centre to end 1.5 times its size);
- the trapeze hangers each is carried on, every 2.4 m (three to a 2 in
  pipe's length, none on a coupling, one within 0.4 m of each elbow): a
  41 mm strut channel across, on two threaded rods into the crown, and a
  strap clamp over each pipe.

A run carries a tray, the pipes, or both, one on either side of the
crown's middle; at its ends the pipes turn up into the crown on their
elbows, and the tray's cables with them (the measurements:
`references.toml`).

## How it came here

The Utility Halls are "shoulder-wide corridors (1.6–2.8 m) in poured
concrete … a low ceiling (2.35–2.65 m)" (`LORE.md`, *Places*); their
remake gives them the shape of a plant's cableways and service tunnels,
and a tunnel carries what a plant needs carried, as the research's
cableways do. Who laid them, and what they carry, is never said
(`docs/plan/FACILITY.md`, rule 1: appearance, not origin).

## How it fits

`PILLARS.md` 2, *familiar but wrong*: the familiar half, real services at
their real sizes, rising into the crown at a run's ends as risers do. The
wrong thing stays the place's own (the bulge, `docs/plan/FACILITY.md`
rule 5): a run is only what a tunnel carries. It carries no printing of
its own yet (a pipe's contents are F11's, word for word, and never a
way or a level: rule 6). Its colours are dull primer and galvanised grey:
never a safety colour on a whole run.

## What a hand does

Nothing: it is over your head. (The torn note's "don't touch the pipes"
stays a warning, never a verb.)

## Where it would be found

Along the straight corridors of the Utility Halls, from three tiles long:
on their crown, out of the jar lamps' way, a person's headroom under it,
ending where a corridor turns or meets a hall, a door's lintel or a thing
hung from the crown. Never where the moss has papered the place (the
Lobby's look), never in a place left empty, never on the Building's
floors.
