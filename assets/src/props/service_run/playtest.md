# service_run: why could this be here?

What the playtesters answered (frames where the layout lays it, a fresh
`placement-critic`; people's sessions, `docs/research/PLAYTEST.md`), beside
the brief's story, and the rules each difference became.

## The brief's story

What a plant's service tunnel carries along its crown: a ladder tray of
cables, a pair of pipes on trapeze hangers, or both, along the straight
corridors of the Utility Halls, turning up into the crown at a run's ends;
never where a place was left empty.

## What they answered

**Round 1** (2026-11-17; 4 frames, seed 7 level 6 and seed 4242 level 13:
the layout's runs are architecture, not in the prop census, so the frames
are cameras down three corridors and one deep one):

| Where | Obvious | Could be | No idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| pipes along the crown of a long concrete corridor, a hall at its end | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| a tray of cables and the pipes, side by side, to a blank end wall | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| tray and pipes down a dark corridor into the Lobby's yellow | 1 | 0 | 0 |

"This is a service tunnel: the pipes carrying water or heat to the yellow
rooms run through it, out of sight"; "the building's power and plumbing are
bundled through this corridor so nobody has to see them". Its one doubt:
from these distances it could not see where a run ends at a corridor's
mouth, "if it just stops in mid-air … a player who looks closely might ask
what it feeds".

## The differences, and the rules

- None to its story: the rule stands.
- The doubt was already the rule: a run ends before the mouth, its pipes
  turning up into the crown on their elbows and its cables with them
  (`Game::lay_runs`), and before anything standing in the corridor's
  middle (a lintel, a barricade: the blind review saw pipes run into a
  barricade's boards, and a run now ends short of one).
