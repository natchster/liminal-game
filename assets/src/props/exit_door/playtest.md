# exit_door: why could this be here?

What the playtesters answered (`cargo xtask asset sheet exit_door --kinds
core_exit_door`, a fresh `placement-critic` a round; people's sessions,
`docs/research/PLAYTEST.md`), beside the brief's story, and the rules each
difference became.

## The brief's story

The door an office floor has at its far end, the way to its stair: in the
wall furthest from where you come in, whatever the room in front of it
became, under its lit sign. The Loop's way in is the same door with no
sign.

## What they answered

**Round 1** (2026-11-16; 15 frames, seeds 4242, 77, 202, levels 5-9):

| Where | Obvious | Could be | No idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| an office room's or a hall's end or corner wall | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| a back passage or a service corridor | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| a raw concrete back room, a tiled wet hall | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| the Loop's way in, on a block standing free | 0 | 0 | 1 |

"The fire exit, where every floor has one"; "a back passage built to get
people to the fire stairs: it ends at the door because that is where it
was going". In the raw rooms: "the way out of a basement or plant room to
the stairs; a plain wood office door is a bit soft for a raw back room",
and the same of a tiled wet hall. The Loop's way in, no sign, on a
free-standing block: "a cupboard, or the top of a stair core; it doesn't
read as a way out".

## The differences, and the rules

- **A reason the brief missed: the back of house's doors are steel.**
  Where the floor's far wall is in a maintenance run, service tunnels, a
  storage hall, a pool hall or washrooms, its way down is the steel door
  (`steel_door`, its rule "way down"), without the closet's plaque; the
  veneered door stays where the floor was offices, homes, a parlour, a
  play area, a party, a shrine, an atrium or for nothing.
- **The Loop's way in stays an ordinary office door with no sign** (the
  canon: `LORE.md`, *The Loop*): that it reads as a cupboard or a stair
  core is meant; a stair core is a person's reason, kept.
