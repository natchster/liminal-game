# steel_door: why could this be here?

What the playtesters answered (frames of its floor and of the Stairwell's
`--screen stair-top`, `stair-bottom`; a fresh `placement-critic` a round;
people's sessions, `docs/research/PLAYTEST.md`), beside the brief's story,
and the rules each difference became.

## The brief's story

The fire door of an office block's concrete stair: on the one floor whose
way down is the Stairwell, made up as a janitor's closet with its STAIRS
plaque, under the exit sign every way down has; in the Stairwell, the door
behind you at its top and the door out at its bottom.

## What they answered

**Round 1** (2026-11-16; 4 frames: seed 4242 level 5 from two cameras, the
Stairwell's top and bottom):

| Where | Obvious | Could be | No idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| the floor's way down | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| the Stairwell's top door, from the stair | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| the Stairwell's bottom door | 1 | 0 | 0 |

"The fire door to the stairwell: every office floor has one, with the exit
sign over it and STAIRS on the door"; "the door I just came through; stair
doors that lock from the stair side are a real thing"; "the stairs have to
let out somewhere: a push-bar fire exit at the bottom landing is where a
fire stair ends".

## The differences, and the rules

- None to its own story. The exit door's playtest gave it a second one:
  the way down of a back-of-house floor (maintenance, service tunnels, a
  storage hall, a pool hall, washrooms) is this door, without the plaque
  (its rule "way down", for both; `exit_door/playtest.md`).
