# high_bay

An enclosed mercury high-bay, as tall halls were lit
(`docs/plan/FACILITY.md` F3; the research: `docs/research/facility.md`,
*Light*).

## What it is

An enclosed industrial high-bay of the 1950s and 1960s for one 400 W
mercury-vapour lamp, nobody's make. Under its hook, a ballast and socket
housing 250 mm across and 200 mm high; under that, a spun-aluminium
reflector 560 mm across at its rolled rim and 430 mm deep, closed
underneath by a prismatic glass refractor; in it, a phosphor-coated BT37
bulb, 117 x 292 mm (`references.toml`). It hangs on a conduit stem from
the slab, its rim about 4.3 m up. Its light is a cool white: phosphor
lifts clear mercury's green to a white (3,600 K, rendering 49), bluish to
the eye. Aluminium dulled by the years, the refractor's glass lit.

## How it came here

The Habitable Zone is "poured-concrete halls 5.4–7.6 m tall, columns on a
wide grid, high-bay lamps hanging on rods" (`LORE.md`, *Places*): the rooms
took the shape of a plant's hall, and its lamps with it. Nobody hung them
for anyone; who built the place is never said ("Somebody built them; who,
and against what, is never said": *Barricades*). In a hall of any purpose.

## How it fits

`PILLARS.md` 2, *familiar but wrong*: "real places, slightly off", a real
hall's lamp at its real size. Light belongs to places (`STYLE.md`,
*Light*): its colour is its own, in its pool and its glass, and dies with
it. It never takes a colour the canon gives another meaning (the door's
green, the window's orange, the broken lift's red), and carries no
writing.

## What a hand does

Nothing: it hangs 4 m up. It fails on its own (`world::lamps`): its arc
drops out and the hall goes darker under it until the lamp restrikes,
warming up from dim over seconds, as a mercury lamp does.

## Where it would be found

Over the floor of the Habitable Zone's halls, in the grid the tubes would
hang in elsewhere, fewer where the hall has rotted; never under a lower
ceiling (the tubes are there), never on the Building's floors.
