# lift_doors

The brief (`docs/design/ASSETS.md`, *An asset's dossier*). Its
measurements' and palette's sources are in `references.toml`.

## What it is

A passenger lift's landing doors on an office floor, seen from the floor:
two centre-opening panels of hairline-brushed stainless steel, each 572 by
2094 mm and 32 mm thick (refs 1, 13), meeting in the middle of an opening
1100 mm wide and 2100 mm high (a 1000-1600 kg car's: refs 9, 10), in a
landing frame round it, 120 mm at the jambs and 150 at the head
(ref 10), painted dark bronze as the entrances of its era were, set nearly
flush, 75 mm proud of the wall (the playtest: standing further out, it
read "stuck on the wall like a cupboard"); an
extruded aluminium sill with its two grooves at the floor (refs 12, 13);
the hall call station beside it, a stainless faceplate 91 by 171 mm, its
two 25 mm buttons centred 1065 mm up (refs 7, 8, 14); and over the frame
a position indicator, dark: it shows no floor. Prints at hand height and
grey where hands hold the doors' meeting edges, key scratches, the
stainless dulled in clouds where it was wiped and never polished, scuffs
and grime low (refs 2, 6), the sill grey with grit.

Three of them: a working one, a dead one (its button lights for less than
a second; nothing on it says it is dead), and a broken one: its doors
jammed apart on the black of the shaft, one slid further back than the
other, the other kicked in low and its foot pushed back toward the shaft,
hanging from its top track (ref 15), a red lamp over them, DO NOT ENTER
written in red across them while they were shut, parted with them (its
left half on the left panel, its right gone into the pocket but a
letter), soot round the edges (refs 3, 5).

## How it came here

The canon: "**A dead lift's button** (a lift that does not come; a
floor's first lift still does, and is a way down) can be pressed ... Behind
about one in three of them, a few seconds later, a car is heard going by
behind its doors ... Where the car goes is never said, and it is never a
way"; and "**A broken lift.** On about half the floors of the rooms ... a
lift stands broken against a wall: its doors jammed apart on black, a red
lamp over them that will not settle, DO NOT ENTER written in red across
them, broken by the gap, its call button dead, scorch round its edges"
(`docs/design/LORE.md`, *Within reach*). Office floors have lifts, and the
rooms kept them with the floors; where their cars go, and what jammed the
broken one, is never said, and nothing here says it.

## How it fits

`PILLARS.md` 2: a real lift's doors at their real size, their steel
brushed and handled. Pillar 4: they never say where a car goes; what
looks back out of a lift's black belongs to the look you take into it
(`LORE.md`, *How canon reaches the player*): no number on them, the
indicator dark. Nothing glows but the lights: the button's ring when it
lights, the broken one's red lamp.

## What a hand does

What it always did. The working one: "[E] CALL THE ELEVATOR", and it
comes and takes you down. The dead one: "[E] PRESS THE BUTTON", lit for
less than a second (below the Lobby, and not on a floor with rules of its
own). The broken one: "[E] PEER INTO THE SHAFT", a look you take (once a
lift, and not while hunted, in the fog or with your nerve near its end).
The doors themselves are not moved by a hand.

## Where it would be found

Against a wall of the rooms' floors, where an office floor has its lifts:
one or two a floor (the first works, a second is dead), and a broken one
on about half the floors, the Lobby and the floors with rules of their
own among them. Never against a wall narrower than its frame (a pillar:
"standing on their own, with no wall or shaft around them", the
playtest), which the game does not yet check (`docs/plan/MILESTONES.md`
26).
