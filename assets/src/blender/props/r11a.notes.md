# r11a: the marks of the common floors, and the dust (Props P2)

Five placers the census graded `cartoon` (`docs/research/props-census.md`;
reviews 2, 3 and 6): a puddle that was a blue disc with a ring, dust bunnies
that were grey pebbles, handprints that were dark eggs with a scalloped top,
claw marks that were three wavy brown marker lines, and a chalk arrow that
was a vector arrow under "NO" in a bold sans. Four are marks, made as batch
`r07` made its marks: painted soft and uneven on transparent planes with the
decal material (`lambert`, `transparent`, `depthWrite: false`,
`polygonOffset -2/-2`; never `basic_mat`, which glowed in the dark), each
with a source and a direction. The dust bunnies are modelled (`r11a.py`).

Every painter draws on its own stream (`canvas_tex_opt`), varied by where
the thing was put (`vary_by` / `hand_vary`: a hash of its tile and wall
side, no draw): a painter's own stream starts the same on every floor, so
without it every floor had the same claw marks and the same word.

## The real things

**A puddle under a drip.** Water dripping from a ceiling onto one spot
stands there or soaks in. On carpet it soaks into the pile and backing: a
dark wet patch with a soft ragged edge, a darker rim where the dirt wicked
out with the water (r07's puddle, the same physics), and where the drops
keep landing the pile is saturated and water stands on it with a sheen. On a
hard floor it stands as a film: darker than the dry floor round it (less
light scattered back from filled pores), its edge sharp and lobed, the
dried tide lines of where it reached before outside it. A drop landing on
standing water sends rings out at about the speed of the slowest ripples on
water (capillary-gravity waves, minimum phase speed 0.23 m/s at a 1.7 cm
wavelength), gone within a second.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave (minimum phase speed)
- https://news.ncsu.edu/?p=934367, https://engineerfix.com/does-concrete-lighten-as-it-dries/ (why wet is darker; from r07)

**A dust bunny.** A loose clump of hair, textile lint, skin flakes, spider
silk, dust and grit that forms where air does not move (under furniture,
along walls, in corners), held together by static and its own entangled
fibres; grey, fluffy, irregular, from a wisp to several centimetres. No one
measures them: the sizes used are 4-7 cm across and 1.4-2 cm high (an
abandoned floor's, larger than a swept home's), within 13% either way.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_bunny
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lint_(material) (fibres shed by clothing, carpets, towels, upholstery)
- https://www.straightdope.com/21342510/where-do-dust-bunnies-come-from

**A hand's grime on a wall.** An adult hand is about 18-19 cm long (male
median 183-188 mm, female 166 mm), its palm about 8.5 cm broad and 10 cm
long, the middle finger about 8 cm past the palm (Chaurand et al. 2007,
Colombian adults; Bangladeshi potters 185.5 mm x 82 mm). A hand pressed on
a wall leaves its dirt, soot and skin oil where it touches: the finger pads
(heaviest at the tips), the pads under the fingers, the ball of the thumb
and the little finger's side of the heel; the hollow of the palm and the
creases of the joints and palm barely touch. Dirt transfers in patches, and
the hand slides a little as it comes away. A hand dragged along a wall
leaves its fingers' tracks, thinning out as the grime runs off.
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9225906/table/tab3 (hand length, breadth, palm length by percentile)
- https://www.banglajol.info/index.php/BJA/article/view/75506/49897 (hand length and breadth)

**Claw marks in a wall.** Nails dragged down drywall tear it: the paint and
the white face paper rip back in a ragged lip, the face paper's brown inner
plies show at the tear, the white gypsum core is gouged out beneath (and
greys with years of dust), a strip of paper rolls up where a nail lifted,
and gypsum dust falls down the wall under them. A hand's nails are a finger
apart (the fingers 1.6-2 cm wide), the middle ones longest; dug in at once,
lifting slowly, skipping where they chattered. On concrete or brick nails
only score pale furrows with chipped edges and grit; on glazed tile, fine
scratches through the glaze with flakes chipped out along them.
- https://www.askthebuilder.com/drywall-paper-repair-tips (the brown layer under the face paper)
- https://www.protoolreviews.com/how-to-repair-torn-drywall-paper (torn face paper, exposed gypsum)

**A chalk arrow.** Blackboard chalk (a calcium carbonate stick 9.5-10 mm
across, its stroke 6-8 mm wide) dragged over a painted wall: grains catch
on the wall's tooth and not between, heavier where pressed, broken where it
skipped, the dust falling below (the r08 chalk's sources: Dixon Prang
3.25 x 0.38 in, Giotto Robercolor 9.6 x 80 mm). A stranger's way-mark: a
quick shaft, a closed head scribbled in, a word in capitals 5 cm tall.
Never her arrow (open, the left barb longer, `docs/SECRETS.md` 1), never
her low chalk stroke, never the secrets' scratched circle.

Each lump is an ellipsoid pushed into lobes and roughened into tufts; the
ropes are thin tubes wandering out and lying down where they meet the floor;
the foot is flattened on the floor (nothing below y = 0). Budgets of their
own (`auto(…, N)`): 1300, 1400, 750 and 700, 850, 400 (they use 876, 1020,
516 and 576, 720, 288).

## Draws

Every old draw is kept, in order, and now chooses something of the new mark:

- puddle: the chance, the tile, the radius (the plane's size, as the disc's),
  the ripple's phase and speed (when each drop lands), the scatter (3);
- dust bunnies: the chance, the tile, the count; for each bunny its colour
  (which bunny), its radius (its size within 13%), its three scales (its
  turn, its grey, one kept as `let _ = rnd()`), its place; the scatter;
- handprints: every draw of every variant as it was (the hand, the drag and
  its start print, the second hand, the high and low pair, the chalk and
  the scrap, the fade); the scrap's size picks the ball or the wad (and
  scales it 0.9-1.1);
- claw marks: the chance, the wall, the jitter;
- chalk arrows: the chance, the wall, the old flip (which way it points),
  the jitter.

Checked with `--dump-level` on 110 floors (seeds 4242, 77, 7, 202, 303, 1999,
11, 99, 555, 31337, levels 4-14; the census floors of all five among them,
and 7 puddles, 12 clutches of dust, 7 handprints with the drag, the chalk
and the scrap, 4 claw marks, 3 chalk arrows): the same draws on every floor,
and every other placer's objects (placer, pos, min, max, meshes, interact)
the same. Each of these props' own objects keep their positions; the
puddle's box grows by a few millimetres (a square where the disc was a
14-gon), the dust's to the film's 0.46 m square, the chalk beside a
handprint is a group at the origin holding its strips (as r08's chalk).

## Cost

The fluff, its fuzz and the balled sheet's print are made once and shared
(`lazy_static_tex`); the marks are painted per thing, pixel by pixel on every
core (`par_rows`; a chalk stroke's segments sorted into 16 px cells, a
gouge's skipped outside its bounds). `--bench` build time, best of three,
old and new: seed 99 level 4 (claw marks) 306 -> 319 ms, seed 11 level 4
(chalk arrow) 372 -> 374, seed 1999 level 6 (handprint and its chalk) 585 ->
594, seed 1999 level 9 (dust bunnies, chalk arrow) 646 -> 657, seed 202
level 12 (puddle, dust bunnies, handprint) 655 -> 669.

## What the look cannot do; what was left

- No reflections: the water's sheen is the phong highlight of the lights
  near it, and the drop's ring a painted crest.
- The chalk arrow's old flip turned the plane to the wall, where it was
  culled: half the arrows were never seen (seed 1999 level 9 and seed 303
  level 11 among the census floors). They all face the room now; the census
  cameras for those two stood behind the wall, so the evidence adds a camera
  of its own in front of each.
- The marks keep the old planes' sizes so `settle_props` probes the wall
  where it did; the hand's plane stays 15 mm off the wall.
