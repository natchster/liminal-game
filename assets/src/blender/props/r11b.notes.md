# r11b: marks and wear on the walls

Five wall placers (docs/design/PROPS.md, P2), after batch `r07`: peeled paint
(`place_peeled_paint_patches`, p19), peeling wallpaper
(`place_peeling_wallpapers`, p22), rust streaks (`place_rust_stain_streaks`,
p21), mould (`place_mold_patches`, p20) and a leaking pipe
(`place_wall_pipe_leaks`, p19). As in r07 a mark is painted, not modelled:
a transparent texture on a plane laid on the wall (`decal_mat`: lambert,
`transparent`, `depthWrite: false`, `polygonOffset -2/-2`), each pixel a
pure function of where it is, filled on every core (`par_rows`), the mark's
layout from a seed made of where it stands (no level draws). What stands
off the wall is modelled (`r11b.py`) and placed as modelled.

## The real things

**Peeled paint.** Paint on plaster lets go where damp comes through the
wall (or where a later coat was put over a soft, chalky one): the film
cracks into a network of flakes, their edges lift and curl, and they come
away in patches, showing the older coat and, under that, the plaster (or a
board's paper face), often with a white bloom of salts the damp brought out.
A film of three or four coats is some 0.1-0.4 mm thick: its broken edge is a
step that catches the light above it and throws a hair of shadow below.
Flakes curl up and cup as they dry, the top coat on the inside of the curl.
- https://certapro.com/boston-south-shore/?p=3610 (plaster ceiling peeling: calcimine, moisture)
- https://www.specialistpaintsonline.co.uk/blogs/news/paint-flaking-peeling-damp-walls-causes-solutions-uk
- https://www.moneypit.com/how-fix-peeling-wall-paint/

**Peeling wallpaper.** A Euro roll is 0.53 m wide (10.05 m long), hung in
drops butted at vertical seams; the paper (about 150 g/m2, a few tenths of a
millimetre) is pasted with a starch or cellulose paste. With damp or age it
comes away first at a seam or a corner: the corner lifts, turns over and
hangs, its unprinted back to the room, the paste dried on it; the wall
behind is stained with the old paste, scraps of the paper's backing still
stuck, and where it tore the paper's white core shows along the edge.
Paperhangers mark a plumb line in pencil for the first drop's edge.
- https://www.borastapeter.com/uk/our-guides/wallpaper-calculator (roll 0.53 x 10.05 m)
- https://www.bricoflor.co.uk/blog/wallpaper-how-to-calculate-how-many-rolls-are-needed

**Rust streaks.** Water on a ferrous fixing (a bolt, a bracket, a nail, a
pipe) carries iron oxide down the wall: darkest round the fixing where the
rust is made, then orange-brown runs, wider and paler as they go, each
ending where it dried. The fixings modelled: an M12 stud anchor (ISO 7089
washer 24 x 2.5 mm, ISO 4032 nut 18 mm across flats, 10.8 mm), and a pressed
steel corner brace (25 mm wide, 50 x 50 mm, 2.5 mm) on two pan-head screws,
what is left of a shelf.
- https://www.brewers.co.uk/know-how/problem-solver/rust-in-masonry (ferrous fixings)
- https://www.wconline.com/articles/85814-the-finish-line-all-about-rust
- https://www.tikkurila.co.uk/faqs/can-i-paint-over-rust-marks-on-external-walls

**Mould.** The dark wall moulds (Cladosporium, Stachybotrys) grow as
colonies of 1-3 mm specks, olive-black, packed into a sooty, velvety film
where a colony is oldest and thinning to a peppering at its edge; colonies
overlap into an irregular patch, with satellites, often in trails where the
damp ran, over the faint yellow-brown stain of the damp itself.
- https://inspectandtest.net/guides/black-mold-characteristics/
- https://inspectandtest.net/guides/what-black-mold-looks-like/

**A leaking pipe.** The pipe run is batch r06's: 2 in Schedule 40 steel
(OD 60.3 mm) and 150 lb malleable fittings (r06.notes.md). A threaded joint
that weeps leaves a crust of lime and rust under it (the salts left as the
water evaporates); where the joint is by the wall the water runs down the
collar and the wall, wetting a tongue of it, its rims tide-lined as each
wetting dried.
- https://www.moneypit.com/?p=49878 (crust at a leaking joint)
- https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Coalinga_College/Introduction_to_Residential_HVAC_Level_1/08%3A_Basic_Carbon_Steel_Piping_Practices/8.11%3A_Troubleshooting_Common_Issues_in_Carbon_Steel_Piping

## What every placement keeps

Each keeps the old prop's draws, in order (the chance, the wall, the
wallpaper's side, the mould's height, the mount's slide), its position and
turn, and its bounds along the wall and up it (what `settle_props` probes
the wall with). The old decals were never seen by the placement pass (a
plane, depth-write off); the new ones that have solid parts are marked
`floats`, so the pass leaves them as it left the old. The pipe was solid
before and is now: it stays in the pass; its collars reach the old pipe's
ends and top, its stain the old stain's foot.

## Draws

Checked with `--dump-level` on all 272 census floors (10 seeds over levels
4-14, 18 over 6-14), against the old code built with the same mesh file:
the same draw marks, every other placer's objects the same (index, place,
bounds, meshes), and these five placers' 71 objects at the same index,
place and turn.

## What the look cannot do

- The flakes' and the curl's faces are the wall's own material, laid in
  world space: on a curl they show the wall's pattern as it would be on the
  flat, not as it would follow the paper round.
- A drop hangs; the engine has no water to make it fall.
- Found on the way: r06's `build_pipe_run` turns an end that goes "into the
  wall" with `x = -sx * PI / 2`, which by the rotation used here (and seen
  here, both ways) turns the elbow's leg out of the wall, not into it.
