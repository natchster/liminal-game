# r06: the hole in the wall, the pipe run, the posters, the pull station, the coat hooks

Batch `r06` (docs/PROPS.md, P1, the everyday) remodels five wall props of
`crates/placers/placers_p31/src/wall.rs` from real ones. Each part is
modelled at its real size in its own frame and placed as modelled
(`prop_part_as_modelled`, a scale of one). The real things, as built:

## The hole kicked through a wall (`place_cracked_wall_holes`)

- **Gypsum board:** 1/2 in (12.7 mm) regular board (ASTM C1396), its paper face,
  on 3-5/8 in steel studs at 16 in (406 mm) centres
  (<https://www.makingmanzanita.com/stud-spacing/>). The stud is a 362S162 C,
  92 mm web and 41 mm flanges
  (ClarkDietrich S162 submittal:
  <https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/clarkdietrich/Structural-Stud-S162-CSJ-1-58-Flange-Submittal-Sheet-2974513.pdf>).
  The non-structural drywall studs have shorter returns, so they are 6 mm here,
  with a punched knockout in the web.
- **A fist or a kick through it:** the face paper tears wider than the board
  breaks. The gypsum is bared in a ring and broken through its thickness,
  wider at the back. Chunks stay hanging on their paper, pushed into the
  stud bay. Through the hole you see the far board's back paper (grey-brown
  kraft, its maker's line printed on it) and a stud where one happens to be,
  lit only by what comes through the hole. Cracks run out from the blow, and
  the board is dented round it. Chips and gypsum dust lie on the floor
  below. A kick leaves the black rubber of a shoe dragged under the hole.
- **Sizes:** three holes (A a fist, 112 x 92 mm; B an elbow or a shoulder,
  152 x 124 mm; C a kick, 210 x 160 mm). The placer's randomized size picks
  the nearest, scaled uniformly within ±15%.
- **Rendering:** the wall is not cut. The mask (the torn outline, 2 mm proud)
  is drawn before the walls (render order -2) and writes depth only. What is
  inside the wall is drawn before it (-3). Inside the mask, the wall fails the
  depth test and the bay shows. The decal sits between the wall and the mask,
  so the mask hides it inside the hole. The group is marked `floats` so the
  placement pass does not push it out of the wall it is cut into. The settle
  pass still slides it onto the wall's real face. On masonry (concrete,
  brick, tile) the same hole is broken masonry, its edge in the wall's
  material, a shallow pocket behind.

## The pipe run (`place_rusted_pipes`)

- **Pipe:** 2 in Schedule 40 steel, OD 60.3 mm (ASME B36.10). Fittings are
  150 lb malleable iron, threaded (ASME B16.3): a 90° elbow at 57 mm
  (2-1/4 in) centre to end, a tee at the same, a coupling, and a union with
  its octagonal nut. The pipe goes 17 mm into each fitting.
  (Anvil FIG 1101 elbow listing: <https://www.dillonsupply.com/Product/anvil-0310002001>.)
- **Valve:** a 2 in bronze gate valve, threaded, 92 mm end to end, with hex
  ends, a bonnet and its union nut, a packing nut and a stem. Its cast
  handwheel is 115 mm, painted red, the paint worn.
- **Supports:** strut clamps. A Unistrut P1000 channel is 1-5/8 x 1-5/8 in,
  12 ga (<https://www.unistrutohio.com/p1000>). A 160 mm stub is anchored to
  the wall by two hex-head anchors, and a two-piece P1117-class 2 in clamp
  goes round the pipe
  (<https://crescentelectric.com/product/1370187/p1117-df-unistrut-defender-pipe-clamp-2-inch>).
  Where the pipe goes into a wall or a floor, it passes through a split-ring
  escutcheon (115 mm).
- **The run:** 2.8–3.4 m (the placer's draw), its axis 80 mm off the wall,
  0.15–0.35 m up. At each end it turns down through the floor, into the
  wall, or up the wall and into it at 1.0–1.6 m. Where the wall stops past an
  end (a pillar), it goes down. It has a coupling, now and then a tee with
  its branch into the wall, and a union. The valve keeps the old bracket's
  30% draw.
- **Wear:** old enamel (grey, green, cream) flaking off in patches, with
  rust in them and blooming round them. The fittings are rustier, with dust
  on what faces up. Rust has wept down the wall from some joints and dried in
  rings on the floor.

## The posters (`place_weathered_posters`)

- **Paper:** ISO 216 A2 (420 x 594 mm), US tabloid (11 x 17 in) and US letter
  (8.5 x 11 in). Each is held by 3/4 in (19 mm) masking tape across its
  corners. One corner has lost its tape and curled off the wall (geometry,
  its blank back seen in the curl). One corner tore away and stayed under
  its tape. The paper is cockled from the damp.
- **What they say:** each says what a real one of its kind carries, set in
  real type and painted in the canvas. Nothing is about the place, and none
  carries a name or a level number (docs/LORE.md, "How canon reaches the
  player"):
  - a safety poster (LIFT SAFELY, the figure squatting to the box, three
    rules);
  - a dance's two-ink bill (SATURDAY NIGHT DANCE, LIVE MUSIC, 8 PM till
    midnight, $5);
  - a bake sale from an office printer;
  - a recycling notice (PAPER ONLY, YES / NO);
  - two flyers with tear-off tabs (guitar lessons, a room for rent), three
    tabs taken.

  The phone numbers are 555-01xx, the range kept for fiction
  (<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/555_(telephone_number)>).
- **Wear:** the sun's bleaching (the opacity draw), yellowed paper, foxing,
  a damp tide line at the foot, the crease of the fold it was posted in,
  hands' grime low down. The R316 8% variant is in a clip frame: backing
  board, glass and four spring clips. The glass is broken (cracks from the
  impact, rings round it), and its pieces lie on the floor in front of the
  wall (the old shards' draws).

## The fire alarm pull station (`place_fire_alarms`)

- **Real one:** a single-action pull station of the Fire-Lite BG-12 class,
  4.12 x 5.5 x 1.39 in (<https://firealarm.com/product/fire-lite-bg-12/>),
  on its red SB-10 surface back box, 5.50 x 4.13 x 1.37 in
  (<https://firealarm.com/?p=47434>). FIRE is raised over the lever's well,
  and PULL DOWN is raised on the lever (Blender's own face, extruded). It has
  the older stations' glass rod across the lever in two lugs, a reset lock,
  and four cover screws.
- **Conduit:** 1/2 in EMT (OD 17.9 mm). It runs from a set-screw connector
  in the box's top knockout, up the wall on one-hole straps with clamp-back
  spacers, to a 4 in square junction box under the ceiling (kept below
  2.85 m, so the settle pass does not take it for a ceiling fixture).
- **Wear:** red enamel rubbed at its edges, chipped to the metal at the
  corners, grimy round the well, scratched. When it has been pulled (the
  R295 15% draw), the lever is down, the rod is snapped to two stubs, and
  the rod's pieces lie on the floor before the wall.

## The coat hook rail (`place_coat_hook_rows`)

- **Hooks:** cast iron double coat-and-hat hooks, 140 mm tall and 95 mm out
  (Schoolhouse Charter: 5.5 in high, 3.75 in projection,
  <https://store.schoolhouse.com/products/charter-cast-iron-hook>). Each has
  a base plate with two screws, a coat hook curling up to a knob, and a
  longer hat prong at 35° to a ball. They are black.
- **Rail:** old oak, 700 x 95 x 19 mm (the placer's 0.7 m rail), its front
  edges chamfered. It is screwed to the wall through countersunk holes. The
  3–5 hooks are spaced along it as the placer spaces them (300–150 mm).
- **The coat (R264, 15%):** a wool overcoat 0.98 m long, hung by the loop in
  its collar. The collar is rolled under the loop, the shoulders have
  fallen, and the lapels run in a V. The body hangs against the wall in long
  folds. It has pocket flaps, sleeves down its sides with cuffs, and four
  horn buttons. Its colour is the old palette's pick (camel, navy, oxblood,
  loden) at a real wool albedo.
