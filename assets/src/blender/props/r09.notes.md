# r09: the crate, the broken sash, the umbrella, the things put away

Props P1 (`docs/design/PROPS.md`): four kinds the census graded `cartoon`
(`docs/research/props-census.md`), made again as the real things at their
real sizes. Each part is placed as modelled (`prop_part_as_modelled`).

## The wooden crate (`place_wooden_crates`)

The census: "a solid orange-brown cube with four horizontal grooves for
planks, and perfect little dark spheres scattered round it; no gaps between
slats, no corner posts, no grain variation" (review2, sheet098).

**The real one.** A nailed slatted crate of rough sawn softwood: boards of
9-15 mm nailed to square corner posts or battens, gaps between the boards,
a lid of the same boards on cleats; packed with wood wool. Slat thickness
of retail pine crates about 3/8 in (9.5 mm), the sizes of cubic and
near-cubic crates 40-48 cm (Uline's 40 x 40 x 40 in export crate is the
industrial big brother; the 0.35-0.45 m the placer draws is a produce or
parts crate):
- https://www.canadiantire.ca/en/pdp/adwood-solid-pine-wood-storage-crate-20-in-2420048p.html (slatted pine crate, slats about 3/8 in)
- https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/S-20436/Wood-Crates/Wood-Crate-40-x-40-x-40-with-Pallet (nailed export crate, posts and cleats)
- https://www.fao.org/4/x5016e/X5016E0b.htm (FAO: wooden field crates, boards nailed to corner posts, gaps for air)

**Made.** A 0.40 m cube (scaled 0.875-1.125 by the placer's size draw):
four 82 mm boards a side, 12 mm, 20 mm gaps; 30 mm corner posts inside the
corners; four bottom boards; a lid of four boards on two cleats; two wire
nail heads at each end of each board (80); wood wool packed to a little
over half, seen through the gaps. Each board a little out of true, its ends
not quite square, now and then a corner split off. The smaller crate
stacked on it is its own (0.30 m or 0.225 m, three boards a side, 9-10 mm),
scaled within 15%. A stove-in side: one or two boards snapped, a stub
still nailed at each end, the one bent in, the middle gone; a tuft of the
wood wool pulled out of the hole; splinters and tufts on the floor.
Materials: pine weathered grey (four tones), the grain painted (knots,
torn fibres, bleaching, the damp at its foot, each board its own shade), a
shipper's stencil sprayed on the long sides (its overspray, the bridges,
weathered thin); rusty nail heads; pale straw wood wool.

## The broken window (`place_broken_window_frames`)

The census: "a flat grey-brown frame on the wallpaper; the opening a flat,
pure black fill with two small white shards; no depth, no sill, nothing
seen through it. It reads as a black card in a frame" (review3, sheet015).

**The real one.** The lower sash of an old timber double-hung window, taken
out: stiles 50-55 mm, top rail 50-60 mm, bottom rail 80-105 mm, sash
44-48 mm thick, glazing bars about 22-24 mm with a rebate for the glass
(9-15 mm) and the glass held in linseed putty on the outside; its cord in
a groove down each stile's edge:
- https://source.thenbs.com/en/gb/product/04-historic-single-glazed-sash-window/p5SgrzrJpKxEaYd5YMkEXS/8NrkXzx9EA7FARAPzgTURV (historic single-glazed sash: top rail 56 x 44, bottom rail 80 x 44, putty glazed)
- https://www.ukworkshop.co.uk/threads/making-sash-windows-rail-and-stile-dimensions.115947/ (rail and stile sizes; 50 mm stiles, 90 mm bottom rail)
- https://ukworkshop.co.uk/threads/sash-bar-dimensions-for-historical-windows.144044/page-2 (glazing bars, rebates)

**Made.** A two-over-two sash 0.60 x 0.70 m, 44 mm: its rebates cut in from
the putty face, its sight edges chamfered on the inside, the cord grooves;
the putty fillets in the rebates, cracked, whole runs fallen out; the stub
of a sash cord still nailed in its groove, frayed. It leans on the wall
(0.21 rad), its putty face to the room, where the old frame hung. Three to
five shards still in the putty (3 mm glass, jagged, from the old draws:
which edge, where along it, which way it leans, how big), the rest of the
glass on the floor as irregular shards. The paint: white gloss gone cream
and grey, crazed, peeled to the grey wood along the grain, most on the
bottom rail; grime low, mould in the corners of the lights. The damp
streak behind it and the wet at its foot are soft painted marks now (no
emissive dome).

## The umbrella (`place_umbrellas`)

The census: "a short navy canopy blob at the bottom, a very long bare
shaft, and a Y-shaped fork on top instead of a crook; wrong proportions"
(review5).

**The real one.** A classic stick umbrella: closed length 88-90 cm, eight
ribs of 25 in (63.5 cm), a 10 mm shaft, a bent wood crook, a metal
ferrule:
- https://www.ypc.co.uk/product/fulton-commissioner-umbrella-black/ (Fulton Commissioner: 90 cm closed, 10 mm shaft, elmwood crook, ferrule)
- https://www.foxumbrellas.com/blogs/news-and-information/tagged/umbrella-specifications (Fox: 25 in frame, 8 ribs, about 36 in overall)
- https://gedward.aimsmarter.co.uk/product/FARE_Classic_AC_regular_836756 (FARE Classic: 88 cm, 10 mm stem, wooden crook)

**Made.** 0.887 m from the ferrule's tip to the top of the crook: an 8 cm
metal ferrule and its top cap, the furled canopy 0.62 m (its fabric wound
in a spiral round the eight ribs, the panels' folds, fullest two thirds of
the way to the hem, squeezed under its strap), the eight tip cups at the
hem, the runner, the black enamelled shaft, a nickel collar and a 23 mm
bent wood crook. It stands on its ferrule leaning back 0.249 rad, its crook
just touching the wall (the base where the old one stood). The cloth the
old colours faded to a real black cloth's albedo; the drip it left a soft
mark round its ferrule.

## The things put away (`secrets/things.rs`)

The census: "a wood block with a nail in it on the wall, a smooth tan
ellipsoid hanging under it, and a half-disc stuck to its bottom. Blobby,
the parts are not joined, and the object cannot be named" (review3,
sheet063): the tweed cap on its hook. On the evidence floors the census
also shows the red envelope (seed 1, level 7).

- **The cap**: a tweed flat cap, size 58 (the band 19 x 16 cm; overall
  about 26 x 20 x 6.5 cm; a peak of about 7 cm, curved down at its sides):
  https://www.pottersofbuxton.co.uk/collections/brands/products/stornoway-harris-tweed-flat-cap-green-blue
  (Failsworth Stornoway: 9 1/2 x 11 in flat),
  https://shop.shelter.org.uk/products/failsworth-carloway-green-harris-tweed-100-wool-flat-cap-size-7-1-8-58cm
  (size 7 1/8, 58 cm). Modelled as it hangs on its hook (the hook's pose in
  `dress.rs` is fixed, so the part carries its inverse): the peg in the
  crown near the back, the cap hanging from it, opening to the wall, the
  flat top panel and its roll facing out, the peak hanging at its foot,
  the cloth settling the further it is from the peg. Brown herringbone
  with Donegal flecks.
- **The red envelope**: a hongbao, 9 x 17 cm, the size made to hold notes
  flat: https://www.arhfoundation.org/red-pocket-sugar-bag-size-guide,
  https://stationery.lazada.sg/standard-happy-birthday-red-packet-sizes-which-fits-singapore-dollar-notes-best-sg/.
  Two layers of paper bowed by what is in it, its corners lifting; gold
  border and the character for luck, rubbed; faded, dusty, creased; a lock
  of dark hair out of its flap end and onto the floor (26 strands).
- **The cloth bag**: a charm bag sewn shut on its cord, the size of an
  omamori (about 6.5-9.5 x 5-7.5 cm):
  https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_As2001-16-45,
  https://www.edohakuarchives.jp/detail-75166.html. Coarse linen, 6 x
  7.5 cm, stuffed, lying on its back; its mouth gathered in pleats, bound
  with three turns of the cord and knotted, the gathered cloth ruffled
  above the binding; its loop lying loose beside it.
- **The holed coin**: a brass coin 22 mm, 1.5 mm thick, with a 5 mm hole
  (the size of a Japanese 5 yen piece:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_yen_coin), its rim raised, no design
  of any real coin, lying on the loop of string through its hole, knotted.

The other things (mushrooms, dish, seeds, sachet, jar, ration, bottle,
almond water) are as they were.
