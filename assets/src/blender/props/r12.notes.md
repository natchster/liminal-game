# r12: what a building's services screw to its walls

Batch `r12` (docs/PROPS.md, P2, the common) remodels seven wall props from
real ones: the office wall phone, the payphone, the emergency phone, the
lift's button panel, the fire alarm pull station, the fire extinguisher
cabinet and the breaker panel. Each part is modelled at its real size, every
part of a prop in that prop's one frame (the wall at z = 0), and placed as
modelled (`prop_part_as_modelled`, a scale of one). The real things, as
built:

## The wall phone (`place_wall_phones`, `crates/placers/placers_p30`)

- **Real one:** the 2554-class wall set (the Western Electric 2554, still
  made as the Cortelco 2554), the touch-tone office and kitchen wall phone.
  Its housing hangs on a two-post wall jack plate. The handset hangs down its
  front on a chrome hanger at the earpiece's seat, its handle over the keys.
  Under the keys a window holds the number card. Photographs:
  <https://www.prc68.com/I/WE2554.html>,
  <https://public.bht-berlin.de/hamann/telefon/2554.html>.
- **Size:** about 8.5 x 4.5 x 4 in with its handset (the Cortelco listing,
  <https://global.channelonline.com/acme/test895/Products/overview/M024810306>).
  The rest was measured off the photographs against that height: the housing
  123 x 232 mm and 58 mm deep. The keys sit on a 17.5 x 20 mm pitch, about
  13 x 10 mm each, in a well 5 mm deep. The K-style handset is 212 mm long,
  its cups 60 and 57 mm across.
- **Made of:** moulded ABS. Its colour comes from what is left of the chance
  draw: beige, ash grey, black, now and then brown. The keys are dark grey,
  printed white (the letters over the numeral, OPER on the 0), and the hanger
  is chromed. The coiled cord is the set's colour, 4.5 mm a turn close wound
  and drawn out where a handset hangs from it.
- **The three states (the placer's draws):** hung on the hook with the cord
  looping under the phone. Off the hook (25%), the handset hangs upside down
  half a metre down on its drawn-out cord. Broken (5%), there is no handset,
  the cord is cut off a hand's width under the jack, its conductors are
  frayed, and the housing is cracked where the old crack planes' draws put
  it: dark splits with the plastic stress-whitened along them. Its smoke
  (40%) keeps the old wisp's draws and animation; it is a soft painted wisp,
  lit (lambert), not a glowing cone.
- **Wear:** yellowed from the top, grimy where hands go (the earpiece's
  seat, round the keys, the mouthpiece's rest), dust on the top edge,
  scratches. The keys most used are rubbed thin. The number card says
  555-0147, written in by hand (555-01xx is kept for fiction).

## The emergency phone (`place_emergency_phones`, `crates/placers/placers_p19`)

- **Real one:** a ring-down phone, the same 2554 set in cherry red with no
  keypad: lifting the handset rings a set number (Cortelco 255447-VBA-NDL,
  <https://www.thetelecomspot.com/products/cortelco-standard-wall-telephone-with-no-dial-cherry-red-255447-vba-ndl>).
  Its housing, handset, hanger and cord are the wall phone's parts. A blank
  plate (`emergencyPhonePlate`) fills the keys' well, with a printed card:
  EMERGENCY, LIFT RECEIVER TO CALL, STAY ON THE LINE UNTIL ANSWERED.
- **The sign:** an engraved two-ply laminate sign, 200 x 60 x 2.5 mm, red cut
  through to its white core (EMERGENCY TELEPHONE), screwed to the wall 0.2 m
  over the phone with two screws.

## The payphone (`place_wall_pay_phones`, `crates/placers/placers_p20`)

- **Real one:** a single-slot payphone of the Western kind (the Western
  Electric 1D/1D2 and the smart sets built in its housings,
  <https://payphones.telesfor.org/doku.php?id=smart>; a worn one:
  <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Payphone_(2965587685).jpg>). A
  stainless upper housing carries the instruction card in a window, the coin
  entry, the keypad and the coin return. The black vault under it has its
  lock. The switchhook comes out of the housing's left side, and the handset
  hangs there on a stainless armoured cord.
- **Size:** 200 mm wide and 530 mm tall (the upper housing 294 mm, the vault
  230 mm); 135 mm deep (124 mm at the vault). It was measured off photographs
  against its keypad (26 mm key pitch, 17.5 x 15 mm chrome keys). The handset
  is 228 mm long. The placer's foot (0.7 m) is the vault's foot, so the coin
  entry is at 1.18 m, under the ADA's 48 in (1.22 m) for the highest part a
  hand works, and the housing's top at 1.23 m.
- **Print:** the instruction card in Helvetica: LOCAL CALLS 35¢, the three
  steps, coins returned, long distance, EMERGENCY 911 NO COINS NEEDED,
  operator 0, repair 611. This phone's number is (555) 555-0182. The card is
  faded and foxed behind a scratched, cracked window. The keys carry their
  numerals and letters. COIN RETURN and 25¢ 10¢ 5¢ are stamped into the steel.
- **Wear:** brushed stainless with keyed scratches through the grain, the
  ghosts of peeled stickers, and grime round the keys, the coin entry and
  the coin return. The vault's black enamel is chipped to primer and steel at
  its edges and round the lock, scuffed by kicks at the foot.

## The lift's button panel (`place_elevator_panels`, `crates/placers/placers_p19`)

- **Real one:** a car operating panel's faceplate. It is 3 mm brushed
  stainless, 165 x 360 mm, on four tamper-proof (spanner) screws. The floor
  buttons follow the 2010 ADA Standards, 407.4
  (<https://www.access-board.gov/ada/#ada-407>): 3/4 in at least (19 mm
  here, in 27 mm bezels), in ascending order, columns reading left to right.
  Each floor's raised numeral and Braille are on a black plate to the left
  of its button, and the main entry floor has a star. DOOR OPEN and DOOR
  CLOSE are under the floor buttons, and the alarm under them carries the
  bell. The fire service key switch (OFF, HOLD, ON) is at the top under FIRE
  OPERATION and its hat. CAPACITY 2500 LBS 16 PERSONS is engraved at the foot.
- **Floors:** B, 1, 2 on the bottom row up to 9, 10, 11 on the top. The
  Braille is grade 1: the numeric indicator before the digits.
- **Lit (the old 20% LED draws, one per button):** the button's ring glows
  amber (a lamp). The others are dark diffusers.
- **Wear:** fingers' smudges over the buttons, heaviest on the low floors
  and the door buttons, and grime at the edges. The numerals' black fill is
  rubbed paler on some.

## The fire alarm pull station (`place_fire_alarm_pulls`, `crates/placers/placers_p20`)

- **Real one:** the same single-action pull station as the fire alarms
  (batch `r06`, the Fire-Lite BG-12 class:
  <https://firealarm.com/product/fire-lite-bg-12/>). Its parts are placed
  again (`pullBody`, `pullHandle`, `pullLetters`, `pullHandleText`,
  `pullScrews`, `pullLock`, `pullRod`) and not modelled again. Here it is
  flush on a back box in the wall, with no surface box or conduit: the
  cover's back is on the wall.
- **Its lamp (new, `fireAlarmPullLed`):** an addressable station's LED, a 3 mm
  lens in its bezel. The BG-12LX's flashes red while the panel polls it
  (Fire-Lite's installation sheet,
  <https://s3.amazonaws.com/sdepot.com/lit/FIR001/BG12LX_IM.pdf>). It is dark
  between short flashes every four seconds, phased by the old throb's draw.
  The old red point light that lit the wall round it is gone: an LED does
  not light a wall.

## The extinguisher cabinet (`place_extinguisher_cabinets`, `crates/placers/placers_p19`)

- **Real one:** a surface-mounted steel cabinet for a 5 lb extinguisher,
  17-15/16 x 8-1/16 x 6-1/8 in (Top Brand 35GX48,
  <https://sustainablesupply.com/products/top-brand-35gx48-fire-extinguisher-cabinet-5-lb-6-1-8ind-c2677533>):
  0.456 x 0.205 x 0.156 m in 18 ga steel. The door is a full pane in a
  rolled frame on a piano hinge, with a pull. It is white baked enamel.
  FIRE EXTINGUISHER is in red vinyl down the glass, reading up.
- **The extinguisher:** a 5 lb stored-pressure ABC dry chemical one, 4-1/4 in
  across and about 16 in tall (Buckeye 5 lb: 16-3/8 x 4-1/4 in,
  <https://www.webstaurantstore.com/buckeye-5-lb-abc-dry-chemical-fire-extinguisher-rechargeable-untagged-with-vehicle-bracket-ul-rating-3-a-40-b-c/47225614UNTG.html>).
  It has a red drawn steel cylinder, a forged valve with its carrying handle
  and lever, the gauge, the pin through its ring and its tamper seal. The
  hose comes over the shoulder to the nozzle held in a band. It stands on
  the cabinet's floor, turned so its label and gauge face out past the door's
  edge.
- **Print:** the label in Helvetica: FIRE EXTINGUISHER, MULTIPURPOSE DRY
  CHEMICAL, the A, B and C class symbols, RATING 2-A:10-B:C, the four steps
  to operate, RECHARGE IMMEDIATELY AFTER ANY USE. The gauge's needle has
  fallen into RECHARGE. The yellow inspection tag hangs from the neck, its
  months punched until they stopped and initialled in pen.
- **Wear:** the enamel has gone cream and is chipped to the steel at the
  corners and the door's edge, with rust in the low chips weeping down.
  There is grime where the door is pulled. The glass has a dust film, a
  hand's smear and old tape's glue, and a few letters are peeling. The
  cylinder is scuffed and chipped low.
- **Proud of the wall:** 156 mm, as the real one. The old box stood 40 mm
  out of the wall with 40 mm inside it, and the placement pass pushed it
  out. The real cabinet sits on the wall.

## The breaker panel (`place_fuse_boxes`, `crates/placers/placers_p19`)

- **Real one:** a 6-space main lug load centre on the surface, 12-9/16 in
  tall and 4.2 in deep (a Homeline 100 A, 6-space surface unit,
  <https://www.acehardware.com/p/34038>): 0.215 x 0.320 m. Its 0.083 m
  enclosure has its knockouts on top. The front cover has a dead front and
  the door over the breakers, hinged at its left edge. Six single-pole
  breakers sit in two columns, 1-3-5 down the left and 2-4-6 down the right.
- **The old draws:** each breaker's handle is tipped on or off by its old
  switch's draw (the six in order). The door hangs open (106 degrees, so its
  card faces out). The old door stood open 34 degrees.
- **Print:** the circuit numbers by the breakers, and the rating label (LOAD
  CENTER, 120/240 VAC 1 PH 3 W 100 A MAX, 6 SPACES, USE COPPER WIRE ONLY).
  DANGER HIGH VOLTAGE is at the foot. Inside the door is the circuit
  directory card, its circuits written in by hand (HALL LIGHTS, RECEPT.
  NORTH, COPIER struck out for FRIDGE, COFFEE / SINK, EXIT SIGNS, SPARE).
- **Wear:** grey enamel (ANSI 61) with rust blooming from the cover's screws
  and weeping down, grime where the door is pulled, dust on top, scratches.

## Draws

Every placer keeps its draws call for call: the same calls in the same
order, their values used for the new prop's variety (the wall phone's
colour from the chance draw, its cracks from the old crack planes' draws,
the breakers' handles from the old switches' tilts, the lit rings from the
old LEDs' draws, the LED's phase from the old throb's). This was checked
with `--dump-level` on each prop's census floor and on a second floor (see
the report). The draw counts are equal, and every other placer's objects
(placer, pos, min, max, rot, kind, meshes) are the same.
