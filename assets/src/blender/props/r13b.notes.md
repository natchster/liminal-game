# r13b: the wall coat hook, the broom, the apron, the rolled rug, the floor rug

Batch `r13b` (docs/PROPS.md, P2, the common) remodels five props from
real ones:

- `place_wall_coat_hooks` (`placers_p28`),
- `place_wall_brooms` and `place_wall_aprons` (`placers_p07`),
- `place_rolled_carpets` (`placers_p29`),
- `place_floor_rugs` (`placers_p28`).

Each part is modelled at its real size in its own frame and placed as
modelled (`prop_part_as_modelled`, a scale of one). The real things, as
built, follow.

## The wall coat hook (`place_wall_coat_hooks`)

- **Hook:** batch r06's cast iron double coat-and-hat hook (`Real__coatHook`,
  the Schoolhouse Charter: 140 mm tall, 95 mm out). It is used as r06 made
  it, not modelled again.
- **Block:** a single hook on a plaster or concrete wall is screwed to a
  wooden block. This one is old oak, 70 x 130 x 19 mm, with its front edges
  rounded over. Two countersunk screws (r06's `Real__hookScrew`) go above and
  below the hook's plate. The block's back is on the wall, 4 mm behind the
  group's origin, where the old plate stood off the wall.
- **What hangs on it (25%):** a hand-knitted wool scarf (`scarfHung`),
  13 cm wide, ribbed, fringed. It is thrown over the hook's arm and bunched
  where it bends over the arm. Its two halves fall either side, turn to lie
  against the wall one over the other, and one end hangs 24 cm, the other
  17 cm. The old cloth's palette pick sets its wool (brown, navy, oxblood,
  camel) and its stripes. The old length draw scales it (0.88–1.12), and the
  old sway draw swings it about the arm.
- **Why not a coat:** the brief named r06's overcoat for a coat on a hook.
  The old cloth was 0.14 x 0.22–0.32 m, and the coat is 0.54 x 1.0 m. The
  settle pass probes the wall's face across a wall thing's width at its
  middle height (`settle_probe`). Where a probe misses, it pins that face,
  which turns a pillar or a partition's end into a full wall. On seed 20,
  level 5, a coat on hooks on a pillar turned the pillar's face into a wall.
  The scarf hangs in the old cloth's footprint (0.153 m across, as tall), so
  the settle pass finds the same faces. Measured on 116 floors with these
  props, base against new (`R13B` instrument, not committed): the anchors
  were identical on all of them.

## The broom (`place_wall_brooms`)

- **Real one:** a hand-made round hearth broom: broomcorn bound round a
  hardwood stick with twine, hung by a cord through a hole in the handle.
  Mother Earth News, "Homemade Broom" (an 18 in handle for a hearth broom, a
  hole drilled in it for a leather or jute cord, the head wound with string):
  <https://www.motherearthnews.com/diy/homemade-broom/> and "How to Make a
  Broom": <https://www.motherearthnews.com/diy/how-to-make-a-broom/>.
- **As built:** the handle is a 24 mm stick, 0.46 m long, its top rounded,
  with the hole across it. A jute loop runs through the hole, over a nail
  driven a little upward into the wall, and is knotted. The broomcorn has a
  folded shoulder, is pulled in by two bands of twine, and splays below. Its
  ends are worn 4.5 cm shorter on one side and bent over from standing on
  it, with straws standing out of the sweep. The broom hangs plumb with its
  head's back on the wall.
- **Its size:** 0.71 m from the nail's head to its longest straw, and no
  wider along the wall than 0.11 m. That is the old broom's span (1.14–1.85 m)
  and width, so the settle pass meets the wall where it met the old one. A
  first draft was a 56 in corn broom with a 12 in face (Harper H100:
  <https://paulbhardware.com/harper-12-in-w-x-56-in-l-natural-wood-handle-contractor-corn-broom-h100>).
  On seed 99, level 9 it hung on a partition's end, the wider probe missed,
  and the partition became a wall. The round hearth broom is the real thing
  the old prop's shape (a stick and a round bundle under a binding) stood
  for.
- **Materials:** oiled hardwood in four tones, dark where hands held it.
  Straw in its own shades, its ends grimed. The twine is faded red, green or
  natural (from the tile, no draws). The cord is jute, the nail steel.

## The apron (`place_wall_aprons`)

- **Real one:** a child's cotton bib apron. Chef's Closet's child bib apron
  is 20 in long x 15 in wide (51 x 38 cm), with an adjustable neck
  (<https://www.chefscloset.com/child-bib-apron-two-pocket/>). The Premier
  PR149 children's apron has self-fabric ties 50 cm long
  (<https://www.pencarrie.com/catalogue/products/pr149/specsheet>). The old
  apron's footprint (0.37 m across) is a child's apron's. An adult's
  (72 x 86 cm, Brakes:
  <https://www.brakesce.co.uk/personal-protective-equipment/workwear/aprons-tabards/red-bib-apron-with-adjustable-neck-strap-72cm-28-wide-x-86cm-33-5-long/p/144526>)
  was the first draft, but its width moves the settle pass's probes, and on a
  wall's end it hung past the corner into the air.
- **Draped by Blender's cloth solver** (`apron_drape`, headless, gravity
  along -y, about 4 s). The flat apron is rows 1.5 cm apart, each row's
  points 1.5 cm apart from edge to edge, so the cloth is even and its edge
  is the outline. The bib is 20 cm across its top, the armholes are cut in a
  quarter ellipse to the waist 17 cm down, and the skirt is 38 cm. The
  strap's two ends hold the bib's top corners 14 cm apart, so its top falls
  into a cowl. One end sits a little lower, where the strap has slid on the
  hook. The rest falls against a wall collider from a little out from it,
  and the waist's corners drop. The solver's crinkle is relaxed a little,
  because twill is heavier than it is.
- **Then, on the settled cloth:** a hem rolled round every edge; the patch
  pocket (two compartments, its mouth gaping); the neck strap from each top
  corner up over the hook's cradle (r06's `coatHook`), turning from across
  the bib to along the hook's arm; and the two ties, 40 cm, hanging from the
  waist's corners in front of the skirt, inside its width.
- **Print and wear:** cotton twill, its colour the old pick faded (the blue
  is a butcher's navy stripe painted in its map). Grease and old splashes
  in clusters run down the bib and skirt, hands were wiped at the hips and
  at the pocket's mouth, the hem is grimed, and the top is faded paler.

## The rolled rug (`place_rolled_carpets`)

- **Real one:** an area rug rolled up for storage without a tube and tied.
  An 8 x 10 ft rug rolled across its 8 ft gives a roll 2.44 m long. At about
  12 mm thick over 3 m, it is 22–24 cm through (an estimate from the
  thickness; rental listings give thicknesses of 0.4–1 in:
  <https://www.acmebrooklyn.com/prop/rc180-uva-8-x-10-area-rug/>). The old
  rolls (2.4 m x 24 cm, 1.9 m x 18 cm, 1.5 m x 14 cm) are an 8 x 10, a 6 x 9
  and a 5 x 8 ft rug rolled the same way. They are modelled at those sizes:
  A 2.44 x 0.24, B 1.83 x 0.18, C 1.52 x 0.14 m.
- **As built:** the outer turn is an Archimedean spiral, its radius growing
  a rug's thickness a turn. Its outer end stops low on the room side, 52°
  up from the floor, with its cut edge. Each end shows every turn's
  overcast side (dark) over the foundation's (pale). The hollow core is
  closed a little way in. The roll is squashed by its own weight, flat where
  it rests, and bowed a little along its length. Jute twine goes twice round
  it at 30% from each end, pulled into the pile and knotted on the room side
  with the ends hanging. The outer end's warp fringe, knotted in bundles
  every 16 mm, falls down its front, matted, some bundles split.
- **Pile out, its pattern woven:** a Hamadan-style rug (the floor rug's
  pattern, without its medallion) painted at its knots' scale (4.5 mm):
  the outer turn shows the end border just behind its outer edge and the
  side borders round its ends. It is faded toward a warm grey, grimed, and
  dust lies thickest along its crown.
- **On top (25%; 5% of those):** the old draws turn and offset
  each roll. A roll on another is laid so its middle rests on the one below
  and its ends sag down that one's sides where it is turned off it (the two
  touching, computed per vertex), kept off the wall. Each roll's group
  stands where the old roll stood.

## The floor rug (`place_floor_rugs`)

- **Real one:** a vintage Hamadan scatter rug, 2'5" x 4' (0.74 x 1.22 m):
  <https://oldnewhouse.com/products/2-5x4-vintage-hamadan-rug-11827>. It has
  wool pile on a cotton foundation and is about a quarter inch thick when
  old (a 2'6" x 3'6" vintage Hamadan listed at 0.25 in:
  <https://www.chairish.com/product/1461347/vintage-persian-hamadan-rug-size-2-6-x-3-6>).
  Hamadan village rugs have symmetric knots on cotton warps, a single weft
  shot, and a medallion and borders
  (<https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hamadan-rug>).
- **As built:** the pile's top is 6 mm above the floor, worn 2 mm lower
  along a path walked across it. A ripple stands where it has crept. The
  long sides are overcast with a cord. The flat-woven band at each end is
  2 cm, with the warp's fringe past it: bundles every 16 mm, 3.5–6 cm,
  matted, some split, some turned back. One corner curls up 1.4 cm and
  another 0.6 cm. The old randomized size scales it uniformly (0.87–1.13 of
  the old area's root), never stretched.
- **The peeled corner (25%):** the old draws (its size, its lift, its
  corner) roll that corner of the rug's own mesh up over a curl
  (`rug_turned_corner`), its back showing. It is no longer a separate
  triangle.
- **Print:** woven, not printed flat, at its knots' scale (4.5 mm), stepped
  on their grid. It has a reciprocal outer guard, a main border of stepped
  rosettes between pairs of leaves, an inner guard of chevrons, and a field
  of small stepped stars in staggered rows. A stepped medallion sits at the
  middle with its pendants and its corners. Four colourings (madder red,
  indigo, camel, brown, with ivory, ochre and green), and abrash in bands.
  It is faded toward a warm grey. The path is worn to the foundation, its
  knot rows showing, with grime ground in. A few moth-eaten patches lie near
  the edges, and old stains have their tide rims. The rug's position sets
  its colouring and marks, so every floor's differs. Its back is the same
  pattern seen through the foundation. The weather's tint stays, softened.
- **What lies on it:** the old table lamp and newspaper, with
  their draws and placement, now stand and lie on its pile, not in it.
