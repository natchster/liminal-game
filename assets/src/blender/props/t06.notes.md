# t06: the garden things (P3, the rare)

Eight placers of the garden shed, the country kitchen and the wall crafts,
remodelled at their real sizes (docs/PROPS.md, "How a prop is remodelled"):

- `place_hanging_plants` (`placers_p05/src/t06.rs`)
- `place_garden_aprons`, `place_garden_gnomes`, `place_seed_catalogs`,
  `place_soil_bags`, `place_bird_baths`, `place_fertilizer_spikes`
  (`placers_p06/src/t06.rs`)
- `place_herb_bundles` (`placers_p07/src/t06.rs`)

The old functions are gone from `garden.rs`, `rustic.rs` and `lib.rs`;
`garden.rs` and `rustic.rs` re-export the new ones under their old paths, so
the registrations are untouched. Every placer keeps its draws call for call
(the comments at each draw say what it now chooses, or that it is kept for
its place in the stream), and every placer that had an animator still has
one (the bird bath's ripple is now its floating leaf's drift), so each is
as live as it was: the placement pass protects it as before.

Checked against `main`'s code (`--dump-level`, a script comparing the logs'
draws, `counts` and every other placer's `objects`) on 1,496 floors: seeds 1
to 130 and 4242, 202, 303, 1999, 555, 31337, levels 4 to 14 (95 placements
of these eight kinds among them). The draws, the `solidsHash` and every
other placer's objects are the same on all of them. On three floors
(109/8, 112/13, 32/7) `main`'s spikes were instanced (all their parts
repeat) and left the dump; with `--flags noinstance` on both sides they are
the same there too. On one floor (43/6) the hanging plant hangs on a
leaning wall and `settle_props` sets it 0.5 mm further off the face than the
old one (its bracket is on the wall where the old arm floated); nothing
else on that floor moves.

## The hanging plant (`place_hanging_plants`)

- **Real one:** a golden pothos (*Epipremnum aureum*) in a moulded plastic
  hanging pot on a three-wire hanger, from a wrought iron wall bracket.
  The bracket is Panacea's curved hanging plant bracket (a flat wall plate,
  a square arm curled up at its end, a scroll under it; for baskets up to
  8 in; <https://www.walmart.com/ip/28488527>; Achla's 8 in up-curled bracket
  is hand forged from square stock,
  <https://www.redhillgeneralstore.com/lawn/Achla-TSH-04-2-8-Inch-Upcurled-Bracket.htm>).
  The pot is a 6.5 in (165 mm) plastic hanging pot, 115 mm deep, its saucer
  clipped under it. Left for years in weak light a pothos goes leggy: long
  bare vines with a leaf every few nodes, the leaves small at the tips, the
  old ones yellow or dried, aerial roots at the bare nodes.
- **Made:** the bracket (plate, two slotted screws, the arm and its curl, the
  scroll), the galvanized hanger (S hook, ring, three wires clipped into
  the rim), the pot and saucer (cylinder-mapped: faded plastic, hard water
  run down from the rim, dust), the dry cracked soil shrunk from the wall,
  the crown (shoots standing and grown over the rim, leaves held up and
  hanging, dead ones dropped on the soil), and four vines (A-D, 0.34-0.62
  m; stem, green, yellow and dead leaves each a part). The game hangs the
  old count of vines (5-8) round the front of the rim. Leaves are painted
  at their real scale: the green marbled with the golden pothos's streaks,
  brown spots, dust.
- **Draws:** the pot colour's draw chooses the pot's plastic (terracotta
  coloured, dark grey, a lighter terracotta, beige); the leaf colour's how
  the plant has fared (a dull green, a dark green, the golden marbling,
  gone pale and yellow). Each vine's four draws: its place round the rim,
  (the depth draw kept), its sideways lean, its phase. The old sway is kept,
  barely (a draught).
- The wall was 8 cm behind the old group's origin (the old arm floated off
  it); the parts are on the wall. The pot hangs lower than the old one did
  (on its hanger's wires) and the vines reach to about 1 m.

## The garden apron (`place_garden_aprons`)

- **Real one:** a gardener's waist apron of cotton duck. The New York
  Botanical Garden shop's: 26 x 10.5 in, 10 oz preshrunk canvas, a centre
  pocket and two side pockets across its front, a twill tape waist tie
  (<https://www.nybgshop.org/products/apron-wasit-apron-bees>); Duluth
  Trading's canvas waist apron, 12 oz canvas, ties 30 in a side
  (<https://www.duluthtrading.com/canvas-waist-apron-11610.html>).
- **Made:** 560 x 290 mm, dropped on the floor: draped by Blender's cloth
  solver (headless, about 2 s; the flat apron its rest shape, let fall from
  a little above the floor, bunched, one end turned back over it, onto a
  floor collider): its hem, the waistband's stitching, the pocket panel
  over its lower part with the two seams that make three pockets, its mouth
  gaping; two 22 mm twill tape ties lying loose on the floor, over the cloth
  where they cross it, one turned over on itself. The canvas is painted from
  above over the heap: its weave, faded patches, soil smeared into it, a
  dried water stain.
- **Draws:** the old colour's draw chooses the canvas (olive drab, a brown
  duck, khaki, the natural, a faded denim blue); its place and turn as
  before.
- The drop: the flat apron starts bunched to three quarters of its width and
  four fifths of its length, in soft waves, and the solver lays it down as a
  heap of round folds (10 oz duck: a bending stiffness of 0.8; at 0.35 it
  lay nearly flat and read as card). The ties are of the same canvas, dyed to
  match, as dirty as the rest.

- His nose is the biggest thing in his face (bulbous, 23 mm), his cheeks

## The seed catalogue (`place_seed_catalogs`)

- **Real one:** a seed house's catalogue of the 1930s, its cover framed:
  Burpee's of 1938 is 9 x 6 in
  (<https://napoleonexhibit.com/store/1938-Burpee-s-Seeds-Catalogue-W-Attlee-Burpee-Co-Philadelphia-INV-P0802_135125766768.html>);
  their covers were printed in colour (the Smithsonian Libraries' seed
  trade catalogues, <https://library.si.edu/subjects/trade-catalogs>).
- **Made:** a stained pine moulding 20 mm wide, 220 x 300 mm over all (the
  old frame's size), 2 mm glass (`t01`'s picture glass), a cream mat with a
  window cut with a bevel (`t01`'s mat), the cover 152 x 229 mm cockled a
  little, a hardboard back. The cover is painted for an invented house
  (WARBECK'S *Garden Book* 1937, Warbeck Seed Co., Columbus, Ohio): tomatoes
  on the vine, a cabbage, carrots and pea pods in the manner of a
  chromolithograph, the paper yellowed, foxed, a tide line. A cover painted
  of its own, not the picture atlas (batch `t02` extends it).
- **Draws:** its tilt as before (the old draw).
- The cover's type is the canvas's serif (Times; the canvas maps a family
  it does not know, Baskerville, to Helvetica) and Copperplate; its paper
  is browned (newsprint of the 1930s), darker toward its edges, the mat a
  yellowed cream.

## The bag of soil (`place_soil_bags`)

- **Real one:** an 8 dry quart bag of potting mix (Miracle-Gro's 8 qt: 17 x
  12 x 4 in on the shelf; <https://www.farmandfleet.com/products/330146-miracle-gro-potting-mix-8-qt.html>):
  a tube of printed polyethylene heat sealed across both ends.
- **Made:** the film 305 mm flat, 430 mm long, lying on its back: its
  section a lens that keeps the film's 610 mm round (so 85 mm high in the
  middle, flattening into the seals), the soil settled to one side, the
  film bunched in wrinkles into the seals, creases from their corners; the
  crimped seals, double film, one corner torn; the soil run out of the
  split in a low ragged heap with its crumbs, bark and perlite, and fine
  soil scattered round it (a decal). The print, laid on from above: an
  invented brand (BRIARWOOD) POTTING SOIL on a dark green film, a
  photograph of flowering pot plants, 8 DRY QT (8.8 L); faded, scuffed,
  creased, dusty, soil smeared out of the split.
- **Draws:** place and turn as before.
- The spilled soil is near black peat with dried patches, bark and a grain
  of perlite now and then, laid on from above over the heap; matte (a Phong
  of little specular): the look settle gives a plain Lambert surface a broad
  sheen, and on soil this dark, under a lamp from above, that sheen turned
  the heap light grey.

## The herb bundle (`place_herb_bundles`)

- **Real one:** herbs hung head down to dry, three to eight stems a bunch,
  tied at their cut ends with string and hung from a hook (UW-Madison
  Extension, *Drying herbs*: 5-6 stems a bunch,
  <https://mastergardener.extension.wisc.edu/files/2016/01/Drying-Herbs.pdf>;
  <https://stacyling.com/drying-herbs-the-easy-way/>); garden sage,
  rosemary or lavender, left hanging for years.
- **Made:** a 1 in brass cup hook screwed into the wall; cotton string
  wound six turns round the cut ends, knotted, its loop over the hook, a
  loose end; three stems of each herb (A-C): sage (a square stem, oblong
  leaves in pairs, dried, curled under and drooping, some dropped),
  rosemary (woody stem, needle leaves folded along their midrib), lavender
  (a long bare stem, a pair of narrow leaves, the flower spike of whorled
  calyces at the hanging end). The game fans the old count of stems
  (5-8) out of the binding, every other one dried a shade darker.
- **Draws:** the stem count as before; the old colour's draw chooses the
  herb (sage, rosemary, lavender, an older rosemary, a sage gone yellow);
  each stem's three old leaf draws how it fans out, how far its head leans
  out from the wall and how it is turned on itself.
- Every part is tinted by one dried surface laid on at its real scale (a
  15 mm repeat): mottled where it dried unevenly, darker veins sunk in the
  crinkle, a pale bloom of hairs.

## The bird bath (`place_bird_baths`)

- **Real one:** a small cast concrete pedestal bird bath (Sunnydaze's
  Grecian, 16 in: 20.25 in tall,
  <https://www.lowes.com/pd/Sunnydaze-Decor-Sunnydaze-Grecian-Garden-Reinforced-Concrete-Bird-Bath-16-Inch/5012964173>;
  typical pedestal baths 18-27 in tall, their basins shallow, 1.5-2.25 in
  deep, <https://www.lowes.com/pd/26-in-H-Concrete-Birdbath-Pedestal/3005221>):
  two castings, a pedestal and a basin on it.
- **Made:** the pedestal (430 mm: a stepped round plinth 260 mm across, a
  column tapering from 116 to 98 mm, its collars, the socket) and the basin
  (410 mm across, its rim rolled, 48 mm deep at its middle, a spigot under
  it in the socket), the outsides lumpy and pitted as cast; 520 mm over all.
  Painted: weathered concrete, its aggregate, lichen and black algae, run-off
  streaks, green at the foot (cylinder-mapped); inside the basin, from
  above, the dried algae's crust, tide lines where the water stood and
  fell, dust and old droppings on the rim; a last puddle of still, dark
  water; dead leaves in it and at its foot.
- **Draws:** place and turn as before; the old ripple's phase draw now
  phases the drift of the leaf afloat (its animator kept: the water is
  still, the leaf turns and drifts a few mm in a draught).
- **Leaves:** batch `t05`'s dead beech and oak leaves (`Real__leafBeech*`,
  `Real__leafOak*`), painted as `t05` paints them (its outline cut out, holed,
  veined: its painter copied, since the placers live in other crates): one
  afloat, wet and dark, two on the dish's slope (tipped to it, along it), two
  on the floor at its foot; which leaf and which side up from where the bath
  stands (no draw).
- The basin's inside is matte (a Phong of little specular): left to the look
  settle's sheen, its pale concrete went white under a lamp from above.

## Parts

All in `t06.py`, each in its own frame (see its docstring); the budgets as
declared there (71 parts, 46,346 triangles). Reused: `t05`'s dead leaves
(`Real__leafBeechA-C`, `Real__leafOakA, B`) in and round the bird bath;
`t01`'s `pictures::moulding_tex`, `mat_tex` and `glass_mat` for the
catalogue's frame, mat and glass.

## Seen along the way

- A face turned down gets next to no light in the game (no bounce from the
  floor): the hanging pot's saucer seen from under it is black, as is any
  prop's underside (the geometry's normals are right).
- The look settle gives every plain Lambert surface a broad sheen
  (specular 0.035, shininess 14): on a dark matte surface facing a lamp
  (soil, wet concrete) it greys the colour out; a Phong of little specular
  keeps such a surface matte.
