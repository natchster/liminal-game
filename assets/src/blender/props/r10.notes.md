# r10: what a floor's weather leaves

The weather extras (`crates/game/src/core_props/weather_fx.rs`, census kind
`core_weather_extras`) but the still floors' cobwebs (batch `r05`): the wet
floors' puddles, the derelict floors' fallen ceiling tile, the electric
floors' cut cable and the haunted floors' candles. Each was a toy of
primitives (a glowing blue decal; box shards, a cylinder "rebar" and a
hard-edged disc; yellow tori, a stick and a star-cross sprite; a cylinder,
two flat rectangles of flame and an unlit ring). Each is now the real
thing, at its real size, worn.

## The real things

**A wet floor's puddle** is batch `r07`'s (`place_floor_puddles`,
`r07.notes.md`): on carpet the water has soaked in, a dark patch with a soft
ragged edge and a darker rim where the dirt wicked out to it; on a hard
floor it stands, a dark film with a sharp lobed edge, a damp margin and a
sheen. Its painter is copied into `weather_fx.rs` (the game crate cannot use
a placer crate); it is lit (`phong`) as the floor under it is, so it no
longer glows in the dark.

**A fallen suspended-ceiling tile.** Armstrong Fine Fissured 1728: a
mineral-fibre lay-in tile, 24 x 24 x 5/8 in (610 x 610 x 15.9 mm), square
edge, its face painted white and punched with short fissures and pinholes,
its back the unpainted fibre (mineral wool and perlite, grey-tan, the dust
of years above the ceiling on it).
- https://direct-www.armstrongceilings.com/pdbupimages-clg/1728BL225268.pdf/download/data-sheet-fine-fissured.pdf

A tile that falls (most often wet and heavy from a leak, its ring stain on
it) hits the floor flat and breaks from where it struck into a few pieces,
each keeping a corner of the tile's square factory edges, the breaks
fibrous and ragged, the core crumbling back under the painted face; crumbs
and a white powder are thrown out round it.

The grid it sat in: Armstrong Prelude XL 15/16 in exposed tee, the 2 ft
cross tee XL7328: 24 x 15/16 x 1-3/8 in (610 x 23.8 x 34.9 mm), hot-dipped
galvanized steel, its face capped in white painted steel, a hemmed flange, a
double web with a bulb along its top, stab tabs at its ends. Pulled down
with the tile, it bends where it was loaded, the web buckling there.
- https://www.armstrongceilings.com/content/dam/armstrongceilings/commercial/north-america/data-sheets/prelude-xl-data-sheet.pdf
- https://www.armstrongceilings.com/residential/en-us/suspended-ceiling-systems/prelude-xl-grid-system/item/XL7328BL.html

**A cut length of building cable.** Southwire Romex SIMpull NM-B 12/2 with
ground: two 12 AWG solid copper conductors (2.05 mm) insulated in PVC and
nylon (about 3 mm), black and white, and a bare 12 AWG ground wrapped in
paper, under a flat PVC jacket 0.422 x 0.190 in (10.7 x 4.8 mm), yellow
for 12 AWG (white for 14 AWG). Cut off a coil it lies in loose loops (the
coil's memory) and is stiff enough for its end to stand bent up; an
electrician strips the jacket back several inches and each conductor's end
to bare copper. Where live bared ends nearly touch, an arc jumps: a brief
blue-white flash.
- https://southwire.com/wire-cable/building-wire/romex-brand-simpull-type-nm-b-cable/p/28828255
- https://media.iewc.com/SpecificationSheets/Southwire_ROMEX_TypeNM-B_SS.pdf

**A pillar candle burnt down.** A 3 x 6 in pillar (76 mm across, 152 mm
tall), ivory paraffin. Burnt for hours it has gone down by a third or more,
the flame in a crater: the pool of melted wax at its foot, the unmelted rim
standing round it (on a pillar burnt in short sittings, tunnelled: a high
rim), lower where it slumped and the wax ran over, the runs down its side
ending in drops and spreading round its foot on the floor. The wick, a
braided cotton wick, stands 8-10 mm out of the pool, its tip curled over
into the flame and knobbed with carbon. The flame of a pillar wick is a
teardrop about 10-12 mm wide and 30-40 mm tall: blue in a cup at its foot
round the dark of the wick, yellow-white through its middle, orange at its
edge and its tip.
- https://goodlightcandles.com/products/3-x-6-pillar
- https://pysanky.info/Chemistry/Candle_Flame.html (the zones of a candle flame)

other thing is lit.

## What each function makes now

- `tail_wet_puddles`: each puddle a plane of the old decal's area
  (`2.5 rx x 2.5 rz`, a little longer than wide), `r07`'s painter on it as
  the floor under it is (carpet, or concrete / tile), at 3 mm.
- `tail_derelict_debris`: each fallen tile a group: three pieces (one of
  four missing, by the first shard's size draw), each where it lay in the
  tile slid out by 30% and turned a little, one in five face down; a crumb
  out along the break past each; the cross tee in the missing piece's
  quarter, across it, moved out until clear of the pieces; the dust.
- `tail_electric_wires`: each cable a group: the jacket (which length by the
  first old wire's turn draw, which colour by the second's), the two
  conductors, the copper, the paper; the arc's flash at the bared ends
  (0, 0.068, 0); the light and the animator (the flash's schedule, the rare
  flare, its sounds) as they were.
- `tail_haunted_candles`: each candle a group: the candle (which of the two,
  and which way round, from where it stands: no draw of its own), its pool,
  the wick on it, the flame and its core over the wick's tip, the light
  0.15 m over the pool, the wax spill on the floor. The flame turns to the
  eye about its own upright (`candle_frame`); the interactables (BLOW OUT,
  RELIGHT), the record and the animator's flicker, fade and wick glow are as
  they were.

## Draws

Every old draw on the level's stream is kept, in order: the loop bounds,
`s_take`, the jitter and turn, and in the derelict piles the three shards'
seven draws each and the rebar's seven, in the electric bundles the three
wires' five each and the tip's one, and the flash schedule's first draw.
Each now chooses something of the new thing (the comments at each say
what), or is kept for its draw. Checked with `--dump-level` on seeds 4242,
1, 99 and 7, levels 4-14 (44 floors, every weather): the same draws on every
floor, and every other placer's objects (placer, pos, min, max, rot, kind)
the same.

Build time: a derelict floor's first fallen tile paints the shared face,
fibre and dust once (21 ms, then 0.2 ms a floor); a wet floor's puddles 4-5
ms (their rows painted on every core, `par_rows`); a haunted floor's candles
1.5 ms.

## What the look cannot do

- A wet floor's puddle is placed before the floor's pool room is built
  (`tail_pool_room` comes after the weather extras in `place_props_tail`),
  so one can land where the pool's water later lies, a dark film on the
  water (seed 4242 level 9); the old one glowed blue there.
- No shadows: the floor round a candle is lit right up to its foot, where
  the candle would shade it; the wax spread round its foot is turned a
  little away from the flame to stand for it.
- The arc's light is the old warm one (as asked): on the floor round it the
  flash reads white more than blue.
