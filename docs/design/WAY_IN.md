# The way in: the Jahamun Tunnel

The first minutes of a run, before the Building: Seoul, at night, the
walkway of the Jahamun Tunnel (자하문터널), where she walked every night
with a measuring wheel until she did not come back. You come down the long
stairs on the stone wall, through the arch and onto the walkway behind the
glass, and you walk it the way she did. It does not end. When you have
stopped expecting it to, there is a door in its tiled wall that is not on
any plan, and her note beside it. Through the door is the Building.

**One sentence:** a real place, measured, that does not end; the one stretch
of the game where you know why you are here, and say so.

The canon is [`LORE.md`](LORE.md) (*The way in*); the place itself, how it
is built and lit, is [`crates/jahamun/README.md`](../../crates/jahamun/README.md).

## Why it is here

- **Pillar 2, familiar but wrong:** "architecture follows real offices,
  halls and tunnels: real ceiling heights, real doorways, real materials,
  then one thing wrong." The walkway is a real one, made from its own
  numbers and photographs; the one thing wrong is that it goes on.
- **Pillar 1, emptiness is the threat:** a walkway at night with nobody on
  it, the road behind the glass going quiet; nothing here can hurt you, and
  it is still too long.
- **Monotony** (the canon's second pillar of the place): the same 2 m bay
  of light and steel, the same plate, the same dead bar coming round.
- **Pillar 3, the sound never stops:** the bars' hum, bar after bar; the
  cars behind the glass, fewer, and then none.
- **Pillar 4, you are never shown everything:** the tunnel is never
  explained. Your voice tells only what you know of her, and the door is
  never said to be anything but a door.
- It gives the run a reason before the descent begins: someone came this
  way, and you came after her. Her trail in the Building starts from her
  note by the door.

## What happens

1. **New descent** from the title fades to black and the place comes up:
   the street above the stairs, at night. The camera is borrowed (a look
   you take, `CUTSCENES.md`): along the street, down the two long flights
   on the stone wall, across the red plaza, through the arch and onto the
   walkway, and back into your eyes a few bays in. Your hands come back
   there. Your voice has already begun (*Your voice*, below).
2. **You walk.** The walkway is as the place is: red paint worn to
   concrete, the white tiles, the glass and the road beyond it, a bar of
   light every 2 m. Your lamp stays off: the tunnel is lit.
3. **It does not end.** Past the first stretch the walkway is twenty
   modules (120 m) laid over and over: the same escape plate with the same
   number, the same dead bar, the same jet fans over the road. Once you are
   deep enough that the arch behind you is lost in the haze, the way back
   goes on as well; the arch is not there again. Nothing says so; your
   voice notices late, after you could have.
4. **The door.** Under the escape plate a grey steel door is set in the
   tiled wall; it is locked (E rattles it). After a minute or two of
   walking (60–120 s of time you spent moving, the run's own), the next
   time it comes round it is another door: older, its paint gone to rust at
   the foot, standing a hand's width open on a cold grey light, and a sheet
   of paper taped to the tiles beside it.
5. **Her note** (E: take it) is the first of her paper: in pencil, folded
   twice, in her hand. It goes into her notebook (J) before her pages (it
   is not one of them: the end's card counts her pages, not it), and a
   line of it stays on screen as the thread. You may go through the door
   without it; then it is simply not in the notebook.
6. **Through the door** (E) is the Building, level 1, entered as a floor is
   entered through a door: the fade, the level card, the floor's line. The
   tunnel is not seen again in the run.

## The rules

- **Not a level.** It has no number, no card, no creature, no danger and
  nothing to pick up but her note; nothing drains (your nerve, your lamp,
  your breath for running are as you begin). It comes once a run, at New
  descent from the title. A run started on a chosen seed or level (the
  tools' and the tests' runs) begins on its floor, as before.
- **Light and fog belong to the place.** Its light is baked from its own
  lamps; its fog is chosen by a light map of where each surface is (the
  bores lit: a pale haze; outside: the night), never by where you stand.
  The haze in the bores is thick enough that the arch, and the end of
  what is built, are lost in it from 200 m.
- **The ring is the place's, not yours.** The same modules follow one
  another whatever you do; walking back is as endless as walking on, once
  the arch has gone into the haze.
- **The title is the game's, not the story's:** it still shows the live
  Well. New descent from it fades to black, and the tunnel comes up.
- **The door changes on its own time, never where you look.** Its time is
  walking time, drawn from the run's seed; it changes only while every copy
  of it is out of sight (behind you, or more than 40 m ahead along the
  wall, where a door in a wall you see edge-on is a line).
- **Your voice** tells, in order, what you know; it reacts, once each, to
  what you pass. It never names her, you, or him; it never explains the
  tunnel or the door; it puts nothing on the screen, and nothing hears it.
  It ends at the door. After the door you know nothing, and the canon's
  rule holds again: no narrator.
- **Sound comes from the place:** the hum from the nearest bar, the cars
  from the road behind the glass (passing, then fewer, then none), your
  steps on the paint in a long hard reverb; behind the open door, a wind
  in a space far bigger than the tunnel.

## Your voice

The persona `you_tunnel` (`assets/src/voice/personas/you_tunnel.toml`;
`VOICES.md`): the man who cries for help and talks under his breath, here
before any of it, talking to himself as he walks, low and steady, telling
himself what he knows. Lines play one at a time with room between them,
in order on the way in's own clock; the reactions are said once, when
what they react to happens and nothing else is being said.

## Her note

Taped to the tiles beside the open door, in pencil, folded twice. One of
its lines is the thread.

> Six hundred and eleven. Then five hundred and ninety. Tonight I stopped
> counting.
>
> I stopped waiting for the end. Then it was there.
>
> He went through here. I'm going after him. Don't come after me.
>
> Thread: *I'm going after him.*

## How it is made

- **The place:** `crates/jahamun` (the set: its geometry and baked light,
  the ring, the doors, the walk; the engine only).
- **The glue:** `crates/game/src/modes/tunnel.rs`, a sub-mode of play like
  the Stairwell and the Loop: the level world hidden, the place's own
  movement, its camera, fog and sound mapped onto the game's, the voice
  and the door.
- **The look you take:** the cutscene `tunnel_in`
  (`assets/src/cutscenes/tunnel_in.toml`).
- **The voice:** `assets/src/voice/lines/you_tunnel.toml`, made and judged
  by the voice pipeline (`docs/engineering/VOICES.md`).

## What only people can tell

- Does the voice sound like a man remembering as he walks, or like a
  narrator reading?
- Do they notice the ring before the voice does (it waits for a third
  pass)? Do they try walking back?
- Is a minute or two of walking before the door too long, or too short?
- Does the door read as hers, and the Building as where she went?
