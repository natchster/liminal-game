# Prop census, round 5: review

The question for each cell: does the prop look like a real, worn object, as
`docs/design/STYLE.md` asks, or like a toy, a cartoon or a placeholder? I cropped
every cell and zoomed into the small props before grading them.

How I applied the grades: `real` only where the prop could pass for a
photograph and shows wear. A prop that would photograph well but is factory
clean gets `plain`. Toys (hula hoop, marbles, jacks, kazoo) are not marked
down for toy colours, because the real objects are that colour. They are
marked down only when the shape is primitive or the parts make no sense.

## Broken (on either side of the question)

- **sheet053 cell 6 (overturned bucket):** the bucket's inside is flat
  pure black with no shading, although the lamp shines straight into it.
  This is a black surface, likely missing or black-shaded inner faces.
- **sheet089 cell 5 (background):** the filing cabinet behind the vacuum
  stands tilted at an angle. It neither lies down nor rests on anything
  visible, so it looks like it is leaning on air or half floating.
- **sheet035 cell 2 (floor rug):** the rug has a wood-grain material. It
  looks like the wrong texture is assigned.
- **sheet041 cell 6 (handrails):** no handrail in the frame. Either it was
  not placed or it sits outside the view.
- **sheet083 cell 4 (story):** a wall takes up the right half of the
  frame and half hides the camp. The camera is placed badly; the game
  itself is not broken.
- **Look, not breakage:** at close range the lamp clips flat wall props
  to near white (011-1 mirror, 041-3 towel, 059-2 poster, 083-2 still
  life). Under it, walls turn saturated (011-5 orange, 059-1, 083-2 and
  095-2 yellow), against STYLE's muted palette. The numeric look gate,
  not me, is the authority on saturation.
- The HUD caption ("Almond water — …") is printed over the prop in
  several cells (017-6, 083-2, 089-2, 089-3, 041-1), which hides detail.
