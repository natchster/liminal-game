Automatic Products was a St. Paul family firm, 50 years old in 1999 ([MPR](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1999/12/16/vending-automatic-products-international)). Dixie‑Narco built the late‑1990s "Beveragemax" glass-front drink line ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie-Narco)).

**Recommendation.**
1. **Hero: a glass-front spiral snack machine in the AP 113/123 mould**, 72 × 38.9 × 35 in. It has the most to look at up close: coils, price tabs, products, keypad, display, coin cup and bin. Lit from inside, it also reads as a glowing window in a dark corridor. The AP 123/133 manuals give every number this note needs, and the era (1990s to mid‑2000s) suits the Backrooms.
2. **Optional second: a closed-front lit-panel drink machine in the 501E mould** (72 × 37 × 35 in). From 20 m away it reads instantly as one large evenly lit rectangle. Up close there is little to see, but it gives the scene its compressor sound. Its cabinet can share most of the snack machine's code.

## 2. Construction, part by part (glass-front snack machine unless noted)

**Cabinet and trim**
- Painted steel cabinet "with legs, specify colour". Factory part names give the palette:
  - AP trim: "True Grit Black" and "Dark Gray"
  - AP side panels: "Sterling Royce", "White", "Dove Gray" or "Painted"
  - AP coin-cup and bezel trim: Black, Dark Brown or Medium Gray ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf), [AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf))
  - Crane top-trim options include "Presidential Walnut", "Teak", "Black Haircel", "Bison Black", "Black Pica", "Dove Grey", "Slate", "Nu White", "Stainless" and "Formica Special" ([Crane parts](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-457-458-764-765-784-787-Parts-Manual.pdf)). A woodgrain trim panel is a period-correct office detail. What "Haircel" and "Pica" textures look like is [unverified].
- Door: black horizontal and vertical window trim, foam door gasket 1/4 × 1/4 in, an "Ace Lock" (tubular **[est]**) and a T‑handle, and a door stop. "Window edging" runs 143 in in total ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)). Taking the door off narrows the machine to 32 in for doorways (AP) and 30 in (Crane, [setup guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Setup-Guide.pdf)).
- **Weight sits toward the back** of the cabinet ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).

**Glass**
- AP front window: **44‑5/8 × 26‑7/8 × 1/8 in (1133 × 683 × 3.2 mm), "hard tempered or Lexan only"**. Chilled machines can have an optional thermal (insulated) window ([AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)). AP 133 offers laminated safety glass, and the thermal-glass door stands 3 in proud of the cabinet side ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).
- Crane 167 uses a curved front glass 38.13 in wide. Where a glass heater is fitted, an interlock switches it off when the door opens ([Crane parts](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-457-458-764-765-784-787-Parts-Manual.pdf), [setup guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Setup-Guide.pdf)).
- **[est]** The 26‑7/8 in window in a 38‑7/8 in cabinet leaves roughly 9–10 in for the control column on the right, after the frames.

**Trays and spirals**
- 5 or 6 main shelves, each with 5 (snack) or 10 (candy) selections. Each selection has its own motor at the back of the shelf. Shelves can move ½ in up or down, and they tilt down for loading. A 4-selection gum-and-mint shelf sits at the bottom ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf), [AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)). **[est]** A 44.6 in window over 6 shelves gives about 7.4 in (190 mm) of shelf pitch; over 5 shelves, about 9 in.
- Product depth per coil (AP 132/133 "spiral depths"):
  - Snack coils: 5‑count 4‑1/8 in, 6‑count 3‑3/32, 7‑count 2‑21/32, 10‑count 1‑7/8, 12‑count 1‑9/16, 15‑count 1‑7/32, 18‑count 1 in
  - Candy coils: 9‑count 1‑15/16, 10‑count 1‑3/4, 12‑count 1‑1/2, 15‑count 1‑1/8, 18‑count 15/16 in ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf))
  - **[est]** Count × depth comes to about 18–21 in, so the coils are roughly 46–53 cm long.
- Crane's current guideline (Merchant, a newer model):
  - **coil outside diameter 2.57 in (65.3 mm)**
  - coil spacing (pitch) from 0.44 in (38-count) to 4.58 in (5-count); 10-count is 2.22 in, 15-count 1.41 in, 20-count 1.02 in
  - columns in 3.88 in (98.6 mm) increments, at most 5 wide
  - product at least 0.1 in thinner than the spacing, with 0.25 in clearance to the divider
  - maximum vend force 7 lb (3.2 kg) ([Crane PDF](https://www.cranepi.com/sites/default/files/2023-03/Merchant%20Vending%20graphic%20%26%20configurations.pdf))
- Crane 167 spirals come in left- or right-hand versions, all the same diameter. A coloured retaining clip marks the count: 6 purple, 11 blue, 13 yellow, 15 red, 20 white, 25 green, 30 black, 38 orange ([setup guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Setup-Guide.pdf)).
- **Wire diameter: [unverified]**, no source gives it. **[est]** In the [Food vending machine](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Food_vending_machine.jpg) photo the wire looks about 1/15 of the coil OD, roughly 4–5 mm.
- Seating and the front end of the coil, from AP:
  - The product's bottom rests on the spiral wire that lies on the shelf.
  - The product must be wider than the spiral diameter or it falls through at the front.
  - The front end of the spiral points downward on the left side.
  - Candy spirals carry a plastic "pusher" about 210° from the front end so wrapper flaps don't hang.
  - Fold the bottom edge of bagged items forward and up.
  - Put soft items on the lowest shelf "making the drop distance as short as possible".
  - Use an 18‑count spiral for small bags ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf), [AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).
- Half-cycle motors turn only 180° and are used for thin items set either side of a centre divider ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).

**Labels**
- Crane 167 price "rolls" sit in slots on the tray front: a dollar roll (1–12) and a cents roll (00–95 in steps of 5), with the word **STOP** printed near each end. Selection IDs are clear plastic snap-on labels.
- Selection codes are a tray letter plus a motor digit: A0–A9 down to F. Snack trays use the even positions A0, A2, A4, A6, A8 ([setup guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Setup-Guide.pdf)).
- AP 113 codes look like "A3, F3" and use "price tabs" ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)). In the [PatchWerk photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vending_machine,_PatchWerk_Recording_Studios,_2007.jpg) the shelf-edge labels read A1, B1, B2. The [Food vending machine](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Food_vending_machine.jpg) photo shows black tabs with white prices next to white number tabs.

**Selection panel and display**
- Crane 167 keypad: letters **A–H and J** (no I) plus **1–9, *, 0, #**, 21 keys in total. Printed legends read **"INSERT COINS OR DOLLAR BILLS"**, **"MAKE YOUR SELECTION"** and **"CHANGE RETURNED BELOW"**. Below sit a coin insert, a coin-return button, a bill acceptor and a card-reader position. The display has 10 characters and also shows "sold out" ([setup guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Setup-Guide.pdf), [programming guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Programming-Guide.pdf)).
- AP 113: a membrane switch panel "housed in a stainless steel panel with individual buttons", with a cast front that holds the display lens. Messages: **"Make another selection"**, **"Use correct change"**, **"Check selection price"**, **"Credit" + amount**, and a scrolling default **"Have A Nice Day"** ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- AP 123 has a 20-character scrolling display ([AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- AP 133 has 2 lines × 20 characters. Standby shows "Have a nice day" over "Credit .00". "Use Correct Change" and "Make Another Selection" each show for 5 s. Selection numbers are 3 digits ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).
- Display technology seen in photos:
  - red LED "AMOUNT DEPOSITED" readout on a black glass-front machine ([PatchWerk, CC BY 2.0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vending_machine,_PatchWerk_Recording_Studios,_2007.jpg))
  - red LED on an office drink machine ([Office vending machine, CC0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Office_vending_machine.jpg))
  - green backlit LCD "MACHINE ACCEPT 0.05 – 2.00 $" ([Food vending machine, PD](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Food_vending_machine.jpg))
  - Whether AP used LED or VFD is [unverified].

**Money**
- The door keys ship inside the coin return cup. AP 113 parts: "Coin Return Cup", "Coin Cup Door", a validator hole as a door option, "Validator Guard", "Debit Card" door option ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- Retrofits are common: the [Out-of-order Coke machine photo (CC BY 2.0)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Out_of_order_Coca-Cola_vending_machine,_Syracuse,_NY.jpg) shows a yellow "DOLLAR BILLS" validator bezel added to the button column.
- A coin-acceptor return lever pivots the track plates apart so that jammed coins fall into the return chute ([patent US5524744](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5524744)).

**Delivery bin**
- The bin sits below the window and works mechanically through a linkage ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)). Parts: outer "Door, Delivery Bin" (black or gray), "Inner Door" (also in steel), "Door Drive Arm", "Baffle, Rear Delivery Bin" and an optional bin cushion pad.
- **[est]** The inner door and rear baffle are the anti-theft parts: pushing the flap in closes the path up into the cabinet.
- An optical drop sensor's IR beams sit just above the bin opening ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)). A stainless-steel deflector runs under the front glass, and the retrofit sensor bolts to the edge of the "delivery bucket" ([AP drop sensor](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Drop_Sensor_Installation_Instructions/AP_Drop_Sensor_Installation_Manual.pdf)).

**Feet, cord and wall gap**
- Four levelling screws in the legs. After levelling, AP has the **front lock-side leg screwed in half a turn so that corner drops slightly** and the door closes. Model this: a real machine leans a little.
- The serial plate is next to the power cord on the back ([AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- Wall gap: Crane 167/168 at least **1 in** from the back wall, refrigerated models 6 in ([setup guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Setup-Guide.pdf)). Dixie‑Narco: at least **4 in (10 cm)**, condenser intake at the bottom of the door, exhaust at the rear, 1‑1/2 in socket on the levelling legs, "DO NOT PLACE ANY OBJECTS UNDER THE MACHINE" ([DN Series 90](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Dixie-Narco/Series%2090/DN-Series-90-Installation-Setup-Guide.pdf)).

**Interior lighting**
- AP 113 and the 120 series have **one fluorescent lamp, "on the top inside of the door"**: the **F18T8/CW** (18 W, 24 in T8, cool white), with the starter on the hinge side and the ballast on the top-front-left shelf support ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf), [AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- F18T8 cool white: **4100 K, CRI 62, 1175 lm, 7,500 h** ([Halco](https://lightingsupply.com/products/halco-f18t8cw-k24-109234)).
- Crane 167: an "upper door light assembly" with ballast and starter, F20T12/CW (20 W), or a 25 W lamp on the 167 ([Crane parts](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-457-458-764-765-784-787-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- AP 133 had moved to a T5 lamp with an Energy Star ballast by 2005 ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).
- LED retrofit: one LED strip taped inside the door, plugged into the old ballast's 120 V lead, with the two black fluorescent holders removed ([Capital Vending](https://www.capitalvending.com/LEDN-20.html)).
- Some machines mount the tube vertically on the hinge side, as in the [Food vending machine](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Food_vending_machine.jpg) photo.
- **[est]** With one tube at the top, the top shelf is bright and the bottom shelf and bin sit in relative gloom. Products also shadow the shelf below.
- In a dark room the lit fronts throw glossy floor reflections and a soft pool of light ([jury area, CC BY‑SA 2.0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vending_machines_in_the_jury_area_(490599612).jpg); [Metairie office corridor, CC BY 2.0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cox_Bldg_Metairie_Vending_Microwaves.jpg)).

**Drink machine (lit panel)**
- **Polycarbonate sign face**, some with a UV-resistant coating "to prevent yellowing"; do not scrub ([DN Series 90](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Dixie-Narco/Series%2090/DN-Series-90-Installation-Setup-Guide.pdf)).
- Flavour cards slide in behind the selection buttons; a T‑handle locks the door.
- Corrosion inside is to be steel-wooled and painted with **aluminium paint**, which leaves silver patches on old machines ([DN Series 90](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Dixie-Narco/Series%2090/DN-Series-90-Installation-Setup-Guide.pdf)).
- Lighting is typically **two T‑12 tubes with a magnetic ballast**, left on continuously ([ACEEE 1998](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/1998/data/papers/0713.PDF)). In one baseline machine these were **two 5‑ft T‑12s at 50 W each** ([ACEEE 2006](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2006/data/papers/SS06_Panel9_Paper10.pdf)).

## 4. Sound, for synthesis

**Compressor (drink machine)**
- Small hermetic reciprocating compressors with a capillary tube ([ACEEE 2006](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2006/data/papers/SS06_Panel9_Paper10.pdf)).
- Baseline condensing-unit run time per 24 h:

  | Ambient | Run time | Duty |
  |---|---|---|
  | 75 °F | 7.2 h | 30% |
  | 90 °F | 10.6 h | 44% |
  | 115 °F | 23.1 h | |
  | 130 °F | 24 h | |

  Baseline compressor draw was 374–508 W across those conditions, and the two propeller evaporator fans were rated 46 W ([ACEEE 2006](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/2006/data/papers/SS06_Panel9_Paper10.pdf)).
- Evaporator fans keep running **2–5 min after the compressor cuts out**, then cycle on and off intermittently ([Coca-Cola patent US4467617](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4467617)).
- On/off cycle lengths in minutes: [unverified].
- A 60 Hz hermetic reciprocating compressor (a larger Danfoss model) turns at 3500 rpm ([Danfoss MT44](https://designcenter.danfoss.com/products/climate-solutions-for-cooling/compressors/hermetic-reciprocating-compressors/mt/p/MT44-9VI)). **[est]** That puts the fundamental near 58 Hz (about 48 Hz on 50 Hz mains) with harmonics.
- "One click as the compressor cycles on or off is normal." A failing relay clicks every 1–2 s; a tripping overload clicks for about 10 s and then is silent for 30–60 s ([OzAppliance](https://ozappliancespares.com.au/repair-guides/fridge-making-a-clicking-noise-4-causes-fixes-2026/)).

**Ballast**
- Magnetic ballast buzz is at **twice mains frequency: 120 Hz in the US, 100 Hz in Europe**. Electronic ballasts shouldn't buzz ([Make](https://makezine.com/?p=70252), [Straight Dope](https://www.straightdope.com/21343252/why-do-fluorescent-lights-buzz)).

**Vend**
- The AP 113 uses a "high RPM DC motor" (geared) that must not be turned by hand ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- A generic 24 V spiral gearmotor is rated **20 ± 3 rpm** (about 3 s per turn, my arithmetic) and ≤70 dB(A) ([listing](https://makerselectronics.com/product/dc-geared-motor-24v-3-pin-for-vending-machine-single-head-spiral/)).
- AP glass-fronts are quoted at under 70 dB(A) ([AP 123](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-123-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).
- With a drop sensor, a failed vend sounds like this: one full turn, a **2 s pause**, a second turn, a pause, a third turn ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).

**Beeps**
- AP 113 "a beep will sound" on an unavailable selection. AP 133 beeps 5 times on "WE HAVE A WINNER!!", double-beeps every 30 s on a health-code error, and the chime can be switched off ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf), [AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).
- Beep frequency: [unverified].

**Not found [unverified]:** frequencies or durations for a product falling into the bin, a coin dropping into the cup, or someone rocking or hitting a machine.

**[est] Synthesis starting points (untested):**
- **Compressor:** 58 Hz plus harmonics with a 120 Hz component. 12 min on / 25 min off. A 150 ms relay click and a 0.5–1 s low "shudder" at start and stop. Fan noise runs on 3 min after cut-out.
- **Vend:** a 3 s geared motor whine with load ticks.
- **Drop:** a 0.6–1.2 m fall into a sheet-metal bin, i.e. a thump plus a rattle of the bin door.

## 5. Mechanics and safety worth knowing

**Stuck snack**
- Items hang when loaded at an angle with one corner behind the spiral; they should sit with both bottom corners in front ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/general/lifehack-prevent-vending-machine-money-drop/)).
- Narrow items fall through early, wrapper flaps catch (hence the pusher), and loose bags get under the wire ([AP 113](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-113-Snackshop-Parts-Manual.pdf)).

**Drop sensors**
- AP Golden Eye II: **12 pulsed IR beams** over the bin opening. If nothing breaks a beam, up to **3 attempts**, then the credit reappears.
- In "Drop" mode the motor stops mid-turn as soon as the product falls. In "Drop/Off" mode, three failed vends in a row disable the whole machine ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)).
- Crane "SureVend" anti-jackpot: after a set number of empty vends it goes out of service for a set number of minutes "to discourage a possible thief" ([programming guide](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Crane_National/167%20168%20Series/National-167-168-Programming-Guide.pdf)).

**Money**
- "Use Correct Change" shows when the lowest coin tube is below its sensor. Change is paid in the fewest coins ([AP 133](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/AP_(Automatic_Products)manuals/Snack/AP-133-Parts-Operation-Service-Manual.pdf)). Rejected or jammed coins go to the return chute and cup ([US5524744](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5524744)).

**Tipping**
- CPSC counted **at least 37 deaths and 113 injuries from 1978 to 1995**, from people rocking or tilting soda machines, with at least 2 deaths in 1995 alone ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/1996/CPSC-Soda-Vending-Machine-Industry-Labeling-Campaign-Warns-Of-Deaths-And-Injuries)).
- Cosio's 1988 JAMA study covered 15 males aged 15–24: 3 killed and 12 hospitalised ([Improbable Research](https://improbable.com/2015/01/20/toppling-of-the-pops-a-sometimes-fatal-quest-for-soda-pop/), citing the paper; I did not read it).
- Full-size machines can weigh over 1,000 lb ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_by_vending_machine)).
- Dixie‑Narco recommends bolting machines to the floor or wall where misuse is likely ([DN Series 90](https://www.vesolutions.co/manuals/Dixie-Narco/Series%2090/DN-Series-90-Installation-Setup-Guide.pdf)).

## 7. What looks fake vs. real

**Fake**
- Toy proportions. Real: 1.83 m tall (taller than most people), a window about 1133 × 683 mm, and a narrow ~25 cm control column.
- Every coil full, every product identical and dead centre. Real: sold-out gaps, angled bags (§5), mixed counts per tray.
- Flat glass. Real: 3 mm tempered glass, or a curved front on the Crane, in black trim with a gasket. Give it smears and a slight specular.
- Even lighting. Real: one cool-white tube at the top, so the bottom row is darker, plus product shadows and a hot strip near the tube.
- Uniform saturated colours. Real: black, dove-gray or woodgrain trim, brown or gray coin-cup trim, a yellowed polycarbonate sign.
- A machine standing perfectly plumb and flush. Real: 1–4 in from the wall, slightly dropped at the lock-side front corner, a cord at the back.
- Oversized buttons. **[est]** From the PatchWerk photo, keys are about 15 mm and arranged as a 3 × 7 grid.

**Real**
- STOP showing on a price roll, clear ID labels, a sticker stack, a taped paper sign
- Scuffs and rust at kick height, silver touch-up paint, dust on top
- A red LED reading "Have a nice day"
- A pool of light on the floor and its reflection on glossy tile

**Renderer notes [est]:**
- No environment reflections, so fake the glass with low alpha, a Blinn-Phong highlight and a smear map that modulates specular.
- Put the tube on an emissive map with a point light just behind the top door frame.
- Bake the floor pool into the tile light map so it stays place-based.
- The red display is an emissive-map texture painted in code.
