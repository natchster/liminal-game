# Writing on the walls: what distressed people's wall writing looks like

Gathered on 2026-11-15 for GitHub issue #11 ("Make better graffiti and
bake it"), by a research agent answering the brief below; summed up in
[`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) section 21, the work in
[`../plan/PROPS.md`](../plan/PROPS.md) (*Writing on the walls*). Every
claim links its source; what could not be verified is marked.

**The brief.** The game's wall writing ("graffiti", `core_props/tail.rs`
`tail_graffiti`) is up to ten all-caps phrases a floor ("DON'T LOOK AT
IT", "HELP ME", "DAY 47") in a jittered single-stroke stick font, in one
of seven hand styles (dark red, chalk white, black pen, aerosol yellow,
heavy blood red with drips, blue pen, pencil), on a 2.6 × 0.6 m strip at
1.1–2.1 m, drawn unlit (`basic_mat`) on a plane 4.5 cm off the wall. The
owner finds colours illogical and the lettering cartoonish: it should
look like the writing of an emotionally distressed person, and be part of
the wall. What do such marks really look like, in which media and colours,
how do they sit on a surface and take its light, and how does distress
show in handwriting?

## 1. Real distressed wall writing

- **Prison cells (Cologne, Gestapo cells, 1943–45):** 1,788 inscriptions,
  "in pencil, chalk or charcoal, often incised with nails, scraps of sheet
  metal or glass"; many monograms, numbers and calendars; long texts in
  short lines, misspelt, addressed to named people; one ends with a date
  added each day, "Today is 4.2.45, 5.2., 6.2. … 10.2."
  <https://auschwitz.be/images/_expertises/fings.pdf> (pp. 176–178)
- **Kilmainham Gaol:** engraved under the last whitewash, or pencil over
  it, some pencil scratched over later; short stacked lines ("LILY GLEESON
  / POLITICAL / PRISONER / 20-3-23"); engraving is read by its shadow, in
  light falling across it. <https://kilmainhamgaolgraffiti.com/graffiti/>
- **The Tower of London:** chalk and graphite the main media; raking light
  found 354 marks where 79 were known: most are faint.
  <https://the-past.com/news/science-notes-writings-on-the-wall-revealing-the-historical-graffiti-of-the-tower-of-london/>
- **Richmond Castle:** thousands of pencil texts.
  <https://archaeology.org/issues/september-october-2016/digs-discoveries/trenches-england-richmond-castle/>
- **Volterra asylum:** Nannetti scratched text with his vest's buckle, in
  a band about 1.2 m high.
  <https://spacesarchives.org/explore/search-the-online-collection/oreste-fernando-nannetti>
- **Hiroshima, Fukuromachi school:** chalk messages on soot-blackened
  walls, to find relatives.
  <https://www.wayfarerdaves.com/hiroshima-survivors-ii-fukuro-machi-elementary-school/>
- **Trapped miners and sailors** (on paper, the same hand): a Sago miner's
  note "written very lightly and very loosely"
  (<https://www.deseret.com/2006/1/6/19931489/final-letters-by-miners-say-they-didn-t-suffer>);
  timestamped entries, "words getting fainter and trailing off the page"
  (<https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna10749747>); the Kursk's, illegible,
  "I am writing blind"
  (<https://www.irishtimes.com/news/soaked-letter-tells-last-hours-of-kursk-crew-1.1112366>).
- **Games and film:** what reads as fake is the dying man's helpful note in
  blood, messages that signpost for the player, neat lettering and one good
  line reused everywhere
  (<https://kotaku.com/cool-it-with-the-dumb-video-game-graffiti-965446561>);
  messages in blood are on cliché lists
  (<https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/487072/11-horror-game-cliches-that-need-to-die/>).
  *The Shining*'s REDRUM is lipstick on a door; the Backrooms' found
  footage keeps to one short scrawl, "Don't move"
  (<https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-backrooms-found-footage>). *Not
  verified:* no developer account was found of Silent Hill's or Outlast's
  wall writing.

## 2. What colours someone trapped in an office could make

- Ballpoints are fed by gravity: on a wall they run dry and skip
  (<https://home.howstuffworks.com/pen.htm/printable>,
  <https://pilot.co.jp/media_english/008/>).
- Blood darkens from bright red to brown as its haemoglobin oxidises, most
  in the first hours (<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3137592/>).
- Black dye inks fade toward brown (seen in fountain-pen ink tests only:
  <https://thepenmarket.com/summers-sunburned-inks/>).
- *An inference, not sourced:* an office has pencils, blue, black and red
  ballpoints, black permanent markers, whiteboard markers, highlighters,
  correction fluid, lipstick, and keys or staples to scratch with;
  correction fluid is the white there is. What reads as fake: aerosol
  colours (there are no spray cans in an office), chalk, red blood that
  never browns, anything that glows.

## 3. How marks sit on a surface

- Graphite reflects light, more with pressure and soft grades, at an angle
  (<https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/9-ways-to-stop-pencil-shine/>).
- Dye ink on uncoated paper spreads along the fibres: fuzzy, branching
  edges (<https://fprevolutionusa.com/blogs/news/ink-bleed-through-vs-feathering-key-differences>).
- Marker and pencil bleed through later coats of paint, leaving ghosts
  (<https://www.specialistpaintsonline.co.uk/blogs/case-studies/case-study-stopping-marker-pencil-bleed-through-with-spo-shellac-stain-odour-blocker>).
- Pressure leaves a groove; the ink's density does not follow its depth
  (<https://reference-global.com/article/10.69525/jasqde.242>).
- An engraving cuts the top layer and shows as a groove's shadow in light
  across it (Kilmainham, the Tower, above); plaster soaked up chalk and
  soot, and the messages read now as faint negatives (Hiroshima, above).

## 4. Handwriting under stress and fatigue

- Stress raises the pen's pressure (the limb stiffens): Van Gemmert & Van
  Galen 1997, Van Galen & van Huygevoort 2000, as cited in
  <https://www.socsci.ru.nl/meulenbroek/Publications/Meulenbroek%20et%20al%202005.pdf>;
  mild stress, more pressure and slightly faster writing
  (<https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-influence-of-tremor-on-handwriting/991012821705302368>).
- Hurried, the letters' shapes vary far more
  (<https://cwlab.ss.uci.edu/files/2011/01/ActaPsych93.pdf>); speed harms
  legibility more than small letters do (<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.07401>);
  after long writing the letters grow
  (<https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/influence-writing-fatigue-handwriting-characteristics-selected>).
- Forty hours without sleep barely changed handwriting
  (<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19360497/>): distress shows as
  variability, not as an illegible scrawl. Anxiety shows mostly in timing,
  pauses and pressure (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12245>).
- Standing close to a wall, lines drift up or down
  (<https://medium.com/graphicfacilitation/whiteboard-tips-and-tricks-92966fdef3fe>).
  *Not found:* a study of writing at shoulder height against lower.

## The game's writing beside it

Seen in the prop census (`--prop-census 3 --census-only core_graffiti`,
seed 4242, levels 5–8, before the change):

- **Colour.** Aerosol yellow ("ALMOND WATER SAVES") and a bright red that
  never browns ("I'M STILL HERE") are the media the sources call fake in
  an office; chalk is a school's. The writing is unlit, so on a wall in
  shadow it stays bright: it glows.
- **Hand.** Every letter is the same polyline, a capital about 0.2 m high,
  evenly spaced on a straight baseline with small jitter, centred on its
  strip: type more than a hand. No line breaks, no size drift, no pressure,
  no crowding, no crossing out.
- **On the wall.** A plane 4.5 cm out from the wall: not in the paper's
  grain, not under its grime, not in its light.
- **Words.** Several phrases signpost or give a rule ("IT HUNTS BY SOUND",
  "ALMOND WATER SAVES", "ALWAYS TURN LEFT", "FOLLOW THE HUM"): what the
  sources call fake, and what `LORE.md` keeps out of every channel (never
  a rule, never the way).

## What to take from it

The rules the work follows (the colours and sizes are the research agent's
estimates from the sources, not measurements):

1. **Media and colours (sRGB):** pencil graphite 75–105 neutral grey;
   blue ballpoint (40–70, 50–80, 120–160); black marker (25–45, 25–40,
   30–45), aged toward (55–70, 45–55, 45–55); red pen or lipstick (130–170,
   30–55, 35–60); dried blood (55–95, 20–35, 15–28), its pools (30–50,
   12–20, 10–18); correction fluid (220–240, 220–235, 205–225), opaque.
   No aerosol, no chalk, no bright red.
2. **A scratch** carries no pigment: the layer beneath shows, and the
   groove. Finger writing in grime lightens the wall.
3. **Into the surface:** every mark is written into the wall before its
   light; nothing glows.
4. **Strokes:** marker spreads 0.5–1.5 mm on paper with a darker rim;
   ballpoint skips (gaps of 2–10 mm every few cm) and is gone over where
   it starts again.
5. **Size:** a capital 2–5 cm in pen or pencil, 4–10 cm in marker or
   lipstick, 8–20 cm with a finger.
6. **Layout:** one to four lines of one to four words, stacked, ragged;
   now and then a pencil block of 6–12 short lines.
7. **The hand:** each line sloping 2–6° and wandering; letter height
   varying 15–25% and growing along the text; slant varying ±8°; letters
   crowding at the right edge, or a word wrapped onto the next line.
8. **Pressure:** heavier and darker at the start and on the words that
   matter; about one message in four fades along its length.
9. **Case:** short warnings in capitals, sentences in mixed or lower-case
   print; now and then a word misspelt, crossed out or gone over again.
10. **Counting days:** dates added each day, or tallies (four strokes and
    one across), in drifting columns, irregularly spaced, sometimes
    stopping mid-group.
11. **Height:** centred 1.2–1.7 m for someone standing; scratches and
    pencil also at 0.6–1.0 m, as if written sitting.
12. **Words:** in the first person, to someone, with dates and times, never
    to the player; few, each once; some seen only in light across them.
