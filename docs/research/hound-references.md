# The Hound: references for its remake

*Gathered 2026-11-19 for the Hound's remake (its body, surfaces and motion:
[`../design/ENTITIES.md`](../design/ENTITIES.md), design 1 and §6), by a research agent answering
the brief below. Every claim links its source; what a source reported is kept apart from what was
measured, and what was computed here or is judgement says so. Nothing found is to be copied into the
game: every picture behind these sources is a reference at most (§7).*

**The brief.** The developer finds the Hound "cartoonishly broken, with too little detail" and wants
it remodelled, retextured and re-animated, creepier and more interesting, from references that fit
its lore. The canon ([`../design/LORE.md`](../design/LORE.md), *Hounds*): an old person on all fours
(hands and feet), starved, rump high, the head hanging so low you see only the back of the skull,
thin hair matted over it; a vest and long johns gone the colour of the floor, torn at the knees;
knuckles, knees and spine standing out; one leg drags; blind, the eyelids grown shut; it hunts by
sound: a lope, a listening crouch with the head cocked, sniffing, a charge, a spring. What do these
look and move like in real bodies?

## The answer

A real adult on hands and feet already has the canon's silhouette: arms and legs nearly straight,
the rump highest, the back sloping down to the shoulders, hands set down without looking, each foot
followed by the hand on its side (lateral sequence; monkeys and apes go diagonally). The wrongness
to add is age, starvation, the drag and the stillness, not new proportions: arms lengthened to level
the back read as an ape.

## 1. The canon it descends from

**Backrooms Wikidot, Entity 8** ([page](http://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/entity-8); Palico22,
rewritten by 1000dumplings; text CC BY-SA 3.0; read 2026-11-19). Of the body it says only:
"humanoids with strange biology, with arms and legs that are built for travel on all fours"; "a
human with long black hair growing on the head"; "sharp claws, and an extremely large mouth with
sharp teeth", "often unseen due to the amount of hair"; "a lot of power when they attack a target,
despite appearing sickly skinny". Of behaviour: "crawling on all fours and mauling anyone that
provokes them", hostile "the moment they see a human", "intimidated when you give direct eye
contact", and "if you hear a growling noise, its safest to stay out of sight". Nothing of size,
face, eyes, skin, clothing, or a gait beyond crawling. Its header is "A sketch of a Hound, artist
unknown" (no licence stated); a second image is chompgwenarts', CC BY-SA 4.0. The game kept the
sickly-thin person on all fours, the hair, the growl; it dropped the claws, the mouth, sight and the
stare-down (`LORE.md`'s attribution). The Fandom version's limp and far hearing
([`backrooms-and-samosbor.md`](backrooms-and-samosbor.md)) are nearer ours; Fandom refused the fetch
and was not re-read.

## 2. How people move on hands and feet

**Reported, peer-reviewed.**

- **Sequence.** Shapiro et al. 2014 ([PLoS ONE 9(7):
  e101758](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0101758), CC BY 4.0):
  "In a lateral sequence walk, a hind limb touches the ground, followed by the ipsilateral (same
  side) forelimb". "Primates are distinctive for preferring diagonal sequence, diagonal couplet
  (DSDC) walking gaits"; "Most other mammals prefer a lateral sequence gait". Five adults with Uner
  Tan syndrome walked 98.6% of 513 strides in lateral sequence, mostly diagonal couplets (limb phase
  25–50%; examples 0.20, 0.28, 0.36); "healthy adults walking on hands and feet in an experimental
  setting use lateral, not diagonal sequence gaits" (earlier adult studies about 12–32%, as cited).
  Walking duty factors 0.61–0.75; one running stride 0.44. Faster, they used lower limb phases. No
  speeds, joint angles or hand placement.
- **Couplets.** In adults, crawling on feet instead of knees (longer hind limbs) mostly prevented a
  trot-like gait; a wider stance, a pace-like one (Patrick, Noah & Yang 2009, J Neurophysiol
  101:603–613, abstract only).
- **Speeds and legs.** Adults on hands and feet show "a large range of kinematic coordination styles
  (diagonal vs. lateral patterns)" and a "considerably retracted average leg orientation", the feet
  well behind the hips ([MacLellan et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00693.2011), abstract
  only); nine crawled a treadmill at 1, 2 and 3 km/h, 0.28–0.83 m/s ([MacLellan et al.
  2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-017-5060-y)).

**Reported, observational** (Humphrey, Skoyles & Keynes 2005, [LSE CPNSS working
paper](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/463), not peer-reviewed; five adult siblings who
have always walked on all fours; written of here as biomechanics only): "the hands are placed palm
down, with the weight taken on the wrists and lower ulnar area of the palm. The fingers are either
arched so that the finger-tips make light contact with the ground or else are raised entirely
clear"; "The arms and also the back legs are nearly straight and stiff during the stance phase, with
the bottom raised high, and the backbone remaining straight throughout the gait"; the knees flex
little; the women splay their feet, the man plants his close together. Hands "heavily callused in
the area of the ulnar palm and wrist", fingers unworn; "his hands anticipated the contours of the
rocks, so that he placed them deftly without looking down". Other adults find the gait "tiring and
uncomfortable even after practice".

**The fastest** (Guinness, a record body): 100 m on all fours in 15.71 s (Kenichi Ito, 2015,
[Athletics Weekly](https://athleticsweekly.com/?p=36315)), 15.66 s (Collin McClure, 2022), 14.55 s,
6.87 m/s on average (Ryusei Yonee, 2025,
[Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2025/9/japanese-man-runs-100-m-on-all-fours-in-blistering-time-after-studying-way-animals-move)):
young athletes trained on animals. Their gait was not found described.

**Measured here before.** The motion model's data crawls on hands and knees, hips at 0.48 m,
0.25–0.6 m/s whatever is asked ([`character-motion.md`](character-motion.md)).

**Computed here.** Drillis & Contini's segment lengths as fractions of stature H, as reproduced in
Winter's *Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement* (Fig. 4.1; not re-read online): shoulder
0.818H, hip (greater trochanter) 0.530H, upper arm 0.186H, forearm 0.146H, hand 0.108H, thigh
0.245H, shank 0.246H. For an old man of 1.68 m: shoulder to wrist 0.56 m, hip to floor 0.89 m, hip
to shoulder 0.48 m. On straight arms and nearly straight legs retracted 20–30° with a little knee
bend, shoulders stand at 0.52–0.58 m and hips at 0.75–0.82 m; the back slopes down to the shoulders
by asin(0.17…0.30 / 0.48) = 20–38°, about 28° in the middle; hands to feet 0.8–0.9 m. Levelling it
takes 0.2–0.3 m more arm: Terry Notary's "foot-long 'arm extensions'" are what let performers "mimic
apelike movements" ([AP,
2014](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2014/jul/11/hollywoods-ape-zen-master/)).

## 3. Starved and old: what shows

- **Where wasting shows** (White et al. 2012, the Academy/A.S.P.E.N. consensus, [JPEN
  36:275–283](https://doi.org/10.1177/0148607112440285)): fat lost at the "orbital, triceps, fat
  overlying the ribs"; muscle at the "temples [temporalis muscle], clavicles [pectoralis and
  deltoids], shoulders [deltoids], interosseous muscles, scapula [...], thigh [quadriceps], and calf
  [gastrocnemius]". How it looks (a dietitians' magazine, weaker: [Today's Dietitian,
  2016](http://www.todaysdietitian.com/nutrition-focused-physical-exams/)): "a depression or pit at
  the temples"; "hollows, depressions, or loose skin around the eyes"; "prominent clavicle bones or
  sharply squared-off, bony shoulders"; "depressions between the ribs and shoulder blades"; "a
  depression instead of a bulge" between thumb and forefinger; "bony knees or a depression in the
  thigh area".
- **Cachexia**: "an ongoing loss of skeletal muscle mass (with or without loss of fat mass)", over
  5% of weight ([Fearon et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(10)70218-7)). What is left:
  "thin, shrunken arms, thighs, and buttocks with redundant skin folds" (StatPearls, [*Severe Acute
  Malnutrition*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559224/), a search excerpt; described mostly
  in children, the "baggy pants" sign).
- **The hump** ([Katzman et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2010.3099), review): thoracic
  kyphosis 20–29° to the third decade, over 40° is hyperkyphosis; in women about 43° at 55–60 and
  52° at 76–80; 20–40% of older adults; with it "forward head posture, scapula protraction".
- **Ears** lengthen with age ([Heathcote, BMJ 1995](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.311.7021.1668);
  numbers not read): behind a hanging head, they show.
- **What the floor does.** The hand-walkers' calluses (§2). Kneeling again and again swells the
  bursa before the kneecap, "housemaid's knee", carpet layers among those at risk
  ([AAOS](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/prepatellar-kneecap-bursitis)). A dog
  whose hind legs fail drags its paw, which rough ground wounds and strips of fur ([Texas
  A&M](https://vetmed.tamu.edu/news/pet-talk/degenerative-myelopathy-in-dogs); pet-health sites add
  scuffed toenails): the nearest analogue found for a person's dragged foot. Photographs of real
  starvation are not references ([`modelling.md`](modelling.md) keeps them private at most).

## 4. Old skin and hair

- **Colour** (Lee et al. 2024, [J Clin Med 13:2500](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm13092500), CC BY;
  colorimeter, 53 Korean women): elbow skin had "the lowest L* and ITA° values and the highest a*
  and b* values", the knee next; with age the forearm showed "the most notable decrease in the L*
  and ITA° values and increases in the a* and b* values": darker, redder, yellower (its numbers not
  read).
- **Purpura** ([DermNet](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/senile-purpura)): "irregularly-shaped macules,
  1 – 4 cm in diameter", "dark purple with well-defined margins", unchanging as a bruise is not,
  lasting up to three weeks, on "the extensor surface of forearms and dorsal aspect of hands"; the
  skin round them "thin, inelastic and pigmented"; over 10% past 50. Age spots: darkened spots,
  commonly on the hands ([Harvard
  Health](https://www.harvardhealth.org/skin-and-hair-health/age-spots-solar-lentigo-liver-spots-a-to-z),
  weaker); sun-made, so few in the rooms.
- **Hair**: greying, loss, thinning, miniaturised follicles, changed curl and lipids ([review,
  Front. Cell Dev. Biol.
  2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cell-and-developmental-biology/articles/10.3389/fcell.2023.1278278/full)).
  Unwashed for years it mats into "a compact mass of scalp hair" (plica neuropathica); in one woman
  of "seeming lack of care", "thick plaques of yellow scale afflicting the entire scalp" and
  progressive alopecia ([Monteiro & Lencastre 2019](https://doi.org/10.1159/000501728), a case).
- **The lids grown shut** are the canon's, not researched; the orbital fat lost (§3) sinks them in
  hollow sockets.

## 5. Old undergarments (weaker sources)

- **Long johns.** The union suit, knitted shirt and drawers in one, was patented in 1868 (an Ohio
  State exhibit, as cited by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_suit)); museum records,
  seen only in search excerpts, describe fine knit ribbed at wrists and ankles, buttons from crotch
  to neck and a buttoned back flap ([Whyte
  Museum](https://archives.whyte.org/en/permalink/artifact103.06.1005)). Two-piece long johns
  displaced it; the canon's are drawers and a vest.
- **The vest.** A 1x1 rib stretches most across its ribs and does not curl at its edges as plain
  jersey does (sewing guides, weak:
  [Seamwork](https://www.seamwork.com/articles/a-guide-to-rib-knit-fabrics)); the ribs run down the
  body.
- **Wear.** A knit's lasting bulge where it is stretched again and again ("bagging") depends most on
  its fibres, then yarn and knit density ([Bouatay & Ghith
  2014](https://jtatm.textiles.ncsu.edu/index.php/JTATM/article/view/4723), abstract); that it shows
  at knees and elbows is common knowledge, not sourced. Soils "can oxidize and become set over time,
  causing disfigurement, weakening and breakage of fibres"; mould on damp cotton "often appears as a
  white-coloured velvety growth" ([Canadian Conservation
  Institute](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/textiles-environment.html)).

## 6. Gaits that read wrong, listening, stillness

- **Foot drop**: the foot hangs in swing; it "can produce a slapping sound when the foot hits the
  ground", and the walker learns "to elevate it higher than normal" (StatPearls, [Foot Drop in
  Obstetrics](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK582140/), [Foot
  Drop](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK554393/), abstracts via Europe PMC); otherwise the
  toes drag, or the leg swings out or the hip hikes (secondary sources). **Antalgic gait**: a limp
  from pain, stance on the painful leg shortened against its swing
  ([StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559243/), a search excerpt).
- **Orienting to a sound** (Brimijoin, McShefferty & Akeroyd 2010, [JASA
  127:3678–3688](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3409488); 31 blindfolded listeners, heads tracked:
  measured): the head starts 0.4 s after the sound (0.6 s with impaired hearing), settles within ±3°
  at 0.9 s (1.2 s); peak about 50°/s for a 30° turn, 130–150°/s for 105°; all undershoot, more for
  farther sounds (one 45° turn settled at 36°). Impaired hearing turns "smooth sigmoidal
  trajectories" into "ones characterized by abruptly changing velocities, directional reversals, and
  frequent fixation angle corrections".
- **The cocked head** (Sommese et al. 2022, Anim Cogn 25:701–705, exploratory;
  [ELTE](https://ttkold.elte.hu/en/content/talk-to-tilt-head-tilting-in-dogs.t.5018)): dogs that
  knew toys' names tilted their heads in 43% of trials when one was named, others in 2%; each dog's
  side stayed the same over two years.
- **Craft notes only** (copyrighted works; nothing taken). Mori's "movement deepens the valley",
  stillness and wrong mechanics, a still upper face: already ours
  ([`folklore-and-imaging.md`](folklore-and-imaging.md) P4,
  [`creature-ai-and-fear.md`](creature-ai-and-fear.md) 3.5). Naughty Dog's animator on the Clickers,
  blind hunters by sound: "alive human beings that have something pulling their strings"; at rest
  "just breathing and maybe occasional clicks", small things added "randomly" ([Naughty Dog,
  2023](https://naughtydog.com/blog/the_last_of_us_part_i_hbo_tv_show_clickers)).

## 8. What could not be verified

The full texts of MacLellan 2012–2017 and Patrick 2009 (refused or paywalled): no healthy adult's
duty factors, joint angles or hip heights were read, so §2's heights are computed. No study of a
person on all fours dragging a leg: the drag is put together from foot drop, antalgic gait and dogs.
The exam's look at each site came from a magazine (the consensus lists sites only). NCBI and the
museums refused the fetch. How the record runners move; Heathcote's and Lee's numbers.

## What it suggests

Sourced where §2–6 say so; numbers in brackets are judgement.

1. **Real limb lengths**: shoulders 0.52–0.58 m, hips 0.75–0.82 m, the back down 20–38° to the
   shoulders, the canon's "rump high". Forearms stretched to level the back (×1.22 in
   `assets/src/creatures/body_hound.py`) make an ape (Notary).
2. **Pose**: elbows straight [175–180°], knees a little bent [10–25°], feet behind the hips; hands
   palm down on the heel of the palm and wrist, fingers arched, tips just touching, so the knuckles
   stand up; a thoracic hump [about 50°], shoulder blades drawn forward, the neck bent so the crown
   faces ahead [0.25–0.35 m up].
3. **Anatomy where the exam looks**: temples, deep orbits, collarbones, squared shoulders, the
   blades and the hollows round them, the spine's knobs, the hollow between thumb and finger, bony
   knees, thin calves; folds at buttocks and inner thighs; large ears.
4. **Skin**: darker, redder, yellower at elbows and knees; purpura on the backs of forearms and
   hands (they face you on all fours); callus on the heel of the palm; a swelling before each
   kneecap; the dragged foot's toes scuffed raw on top.
5. **Hair**: thin grey, scalp showing at the crown, matted into a few wet cords hanging to the
   floor, yellow scale.
6. **Clothes**: rib-knit cotton; [the vest hangs toward the floor and rides toward the shoulders,
   baring the lower spine]; long johns bagged then torn at the knees, the seat empty, the dragged
   leg's cuff worn through; set soil the colour of the floor, darkest where it touches it.
7. **Gait**: lateral sequence (left hind, left hand, right hind, right hand); limb phase [0.3] and
   duty factor [0.7] walking, both falling as it speeds; the charge a run [duty factor about 0.45].
   People walk so at 0.3–0.8 m/s; the charge's 6.0 m/s is about 90% of the record.
8. **The drag**: [that foot's stance about 0.8 of the others'], its swing on the toes' tops, the hip
   hitched to bring it through, a scuff for a footfall.
9. **Listening**: dead still; the head turns after [0.4 s] at up to [150°/s], undershoots, corrects
   once or twice, settles by [0.9 s]; then it cocks to its one side and holds. The upper face never
   moves.
