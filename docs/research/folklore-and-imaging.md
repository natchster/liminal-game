# Creepypasta, internet folklore, analog horror and folk entities: what they look like, how they are pictured, why they work

Research note for the entity redesign. Each entry gives its licence or owner. Where I could not verify something the brief asked about, I say so.

Two findings matter most for us:

1. **Soviet details plus creepypasta clichés is already a parody genre.** On Runet, "Soviet creepypasta" (in English, "Such is life in Moscow") takes Soviet everyday life and uses it to mock the best-known creepypastas. Parodies sit beside the originals on the same sites, and readers often pass them on as if they were real creepypasta ([Mirvoda 2018, Perm University Herald, doi 10.17072/2037-6681-2018-3-138-148](https://press.psu.ru/index.php/philology/article/download/1633/1204/3523)). The Building is Soviet, so its tone must stay documentary, or it will read as that genre.
2. **"Shadowed face + realistic eyes" is on the analog-horror fans' list of tired tropes** ([Long, *Sosland Journal* 2023, UMKC](https://info.umkc.edu/sosland_journal/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Long_AnalysisOfAnalogHorror.pdf)), and wide grins are the signature of the two creepypastas that most clearly turned into jokes (Jeff the Killer, Smile Dog). Our current Smiler (eyes and a grin in the dark) sits on both lines. Part 3 suggests a fix.

## Part 1 — Entities

### A. Forum-born creepypasta (2001–2011)

**1. Slender Man (Something Awful, June 2009).**
**Look:** very tall and thin, in a black suit and tie, with a white face that has no features. In later versions his arms are long and like tentacles. **Behaviour:** he stalks and abducts people, mostly children, in woods and abandoned places, and what happens to the victims is usually left vague. Later stories added "Slender sickness": paranoia, nosebleeds and static on screens ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender_Man)). **Pictured as:** an entry in a Photoshop contest for "paranormal images". Eric Knudsen ("Victor Surge") added the figure to two black-and-white photos of groups of children and wrote fake captions, one attributed to a photographer "presumed dead" (same source). Nobody in the photo reacts to the figure. **Why it works:** Shira Chess reads him as a metaphor for "helplessness, power differentials, and anonymous forces", and notes the stories stay "mysteriously and usefully vague" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender_Man)). In the game *Slender: The Eight Pages* (2012), audio cues and visual distortions "similar to TV noise" warn of his presence, and "looking at him for too long causes a game over" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender:_The_Eight_Pages)). That is the clearest game form of "looking costs you". **Status:** overexposed, and tied to the 2014 Waukesha stabbing. **Licence:** owned. Knudsen enforces his copyright, and the media rights were sold in 2016 (same source). Only the general motifs are free.

**2. The Rake (4chan /b/, late 2005).**
**Look:** humanoid, about six feet tall standing, but it usually crouches or walks on all fours. Very pale skin, a blank face ([Know Your Meme, via search](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-rake)). The best-known passage is a sleeper waking to find a figure "sitting at the foot of the bed" with its back to them. Versions disagree about its eyes ([Creepypasta Wiki](https://creepypasta.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rake)). It was built from crowd-sourced fake documents, such as an in-fiction "1691 mariner's log", and has no canonical image ([Wikipedia, List of creepypastas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_creepypastas)). **Why it works:** an almost-human posture in the one place you should be safe. **Licence:** anonymous and collective. The motif is generic.

**3. Ted the Caver (Angelfire, 2001).**
Nothing is ever seen. An online diary of digging into a tight cave passage, where wind, howling and hieroglyph-like markings grow worse the deeper they go ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_the_Caver)). It is sometimes called the first creepypasta ([Wikipedia, Creepypasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creepypasta)). **Lesson:** escalation through sound and moving air alone. **Licence:** Ted Hegemann's text.

**4. Candle Cove (Kris Straub, 2009).**
**Look:** the Skin-Taker, a skeleton pirate dressed in children's skin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candle_Cove)). **Pictured as:** a fake forum thread of adults half-remembering a 1970s children's show. In the last post, one poster's mother says the TV only ever showed static (same source). **Why it works:** it turns nostalgia against the reader, and the monster exists only as something remembered. **Licence:** posted under an unspecified Creative Commons licence, later adapted for TV (same source). Treat it as owned.

**5. Smile Dog / smile.jpg (4chan /x/, about 2008).**
**Look:** a husky in a grainy, red-tinted photo, grinning with human-looking teeth. Some versions add a bloody hand at the edge of the frame. **Rule:** a chain letter. The image drives you mad unless you "spread the word" ([Know Your Meme, via search](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/smilejpg)). **Why it worked:** human features in an animal face (Carroll's *fusion*, see Part 2). **Status:** comic now. Russian researchers list Smile.jpg among the "deadly file" stories that parodies target ([Mirvoda 2018](https://press.psu.ru/index.php/philology/article/download/1633/1204/3523)). **Licence:** anonymous, and the image's origin is unknown.

**6. Jeff the Killer (2008).**
**Look:** an edited photo of a scarred white face with a carved smile ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_the_Killer)). **Rule:** he whispers "go to sleep" before he kills. **Pictured as:** the image spread mostly through jump-scare "screamer" videos ([Know Your Meme guide](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/who-is-jeff-the-killer-the-creepy-internet-legends-origin-story-explained)). **Status:** a byword for edgy creepypasta, now remembered as old-internet nostalgia (same guide) and the subject of memes and fan works ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_the_Killer)). **Lesson:** a fixed, fully shown grinning face plus a backstory turns into a mascot. **Licence:** "Sesseur" wrote the story. The image has no known source.

**7. Mr. Widemouth (about 2010, "PerfectCircle35").**
A Furby-like thing with a Cheshire-cat grin that hides under a sick child's bed and coaxes him toward dangerous "games", such as jumping out of a window ([TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/MrWidemouth)). The friendly shape with a lethal intent works. The wide mouth is the weakest part. **Licence:** the author's.

**8. NoEnd House (Brian Russell, 2011).**
Nine rooms that grow more frightening, and $500 to anyone who reaches the exit. No one ever has, and the narrator may never have left ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/article/36694-channel-zero-no-end-house-creepypasta-original-story)). It is the closest creepypasta ancestor to "levels". **Licence:** Russell's, adapted as *Channel Zero* season 2.

**9. "1999" (Camden Lamont, 2009).**
A public-access channel (Caledon Local 21) whose costumed host, "Mr. Bear", invites children to his basement ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/1999)). I found no "Caleb's Corner", so the brief's name seems to be a mix-up. **Lesson:** a human inside a mascot, plus an invitation. **Licence:** the author's.

**10. The Crooked Man.**
The nursery rhyme was first printed in 1842 (Halliwell). The malevolent verse comes from *The Conjuring 2* (2016). Internet versions add a tall figure with blank eyes, a wide grin and the head tilted sharply to one side, and a rule: reciting the poem summons him ([The Ghost in My Machine](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2021/08/11/encyclopaedia-of-the-impossible-the-crooked-man/)). The film creature was played by Javier Botet, whose Marfan syndrome gives him long limbs and loose joints that pass for CGI ([Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/how-javier-botets-rare-marfan-syndrome-enabled-his-acting-career-1022027/)). **Licence:** the rhyme is public domain. The film design belongs to Warner.

### B. Photo-manipulation art: Trevor Henderson

**11. Siren Head and the others.**
**Siren Head** (first posted 19 August 2018) is a 40-foot humanoid with two sirens for a head. It lures prey with sound: garbled music, emergency broadcasts, mimicked voices. **Cartoon Cat** (August 2018) is a rubber-hose cartoon that appears in abandoned places. **Long Horse** is a legless, very long creature with a horse's head. **Bridge Worm** (2019) is a long white worm with a fake face that lives under bridges ([Wikipedia, Trevor Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Henderson)). I could not verify "The Hungry Dog" or "Man with No Skin".
**Method, in his own words:** he draws creatures into donated or self-taken photos, blending them in with lighting, texture and colour. Each picture is meant as "one moment from a found-footage horror movie that was never made". He finds eyeless things scary because without eyes a creature's intentions become "completely ambiguous" ([Reactor, 2026](https://reactormag.com/artist-trevor-henderson-siren-head-interview/)). He designs the monster "last to suit the space", avoids "sharp teeth, angry brows, claws", aiming for "slightly off proportions", and he likes deciding "how much to show of the creature" ([Daily Dead, 2018](https://dailydead.com/qa-trevor-henderson-discusses-the-found-footage-style-of-his-fascinating-horror-artwork/)). Neither interview discusses scale. My reading is that the giant works because it is set against real roads, poles and treelines.
**Status:** Siren Head became a meme with many unofficial games ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/a/alex-ocho/siren-head-movie-warner-bros-cregger-duffield)). **Licence:** copyrighted. Henderson refused an SCP version so the character would not become Creative Commons, and there are film deals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Henderson)). Take only the method.

### C. Analog horror (2015–)

The genre imitates late-20th-century TV and tape. It uses low-fidelity pictures, cryptic messages, few jump scares, and distortion that hides modern tools such as Blender and After Effects. Obscured images "leave viewers to imagine" what they see ([Wikipedia, Analog horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_horror)).

**12. Local 58 (Kris Straub, 2015–).**
**"Weather Service"** (2015): emergency alerts tell viewers not to look at the night sky. The hijacker says the Moon brainwashed them, and the episode ends "IF YOU ARE AFRAID, WE WILL LOOK TOGETHER." **"Contingency"** (2016): a government PSA over colour bars gives escalating, monstrous instructions. **"Night Walk"** (2024): a 1977 documentary about "The Woman in Profile", a figure seen in the county ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_58)). **Why it works:** in the GPS short, the orders grow stranger ("turn off your headlights") and the driver obeys. Sam Moore calls "Contingency" "a broadcast that ought to be locked away somehow being shown", and places the horror in "the build-up" ([Dread Central, 2025](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/534205/local58-and-the-birth-of-analog-horror)). **Lesson:** rules delivered as official instructions that turn wrong. **Licence:** Straub's.

**13. Gemini Home Entertainment (Remy Abode, 2019–).**
Fake educational VHS tapes. The creatures are Woodcrawlers (long spider-like limbs, nesting in homes), "Fake People" (erratic doubles), Gardeners, and Nature's Mockery, a plant from "Wilderness Survival Guide". They are shown mostly as limbs, shadows and glimpses, and "Storm Safety Tips" tells viewers to return to the bunker if they see "lights" in the distance ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_Home_Entertainment)). I could not verify any "Walkers". **Licence:** Abode's.

**14. The Mandela Catalogue (Alex Kister, 2021–).**
"Alternates" are shape-shifting doubles that take the form of friends and relatives. They are presented through instructional videos for Mandela County, and the advice is to run and hide if you see someone identical to you ([Fangoria](https://www.fangoria.com/?p=116688)). The "intruder" image is a recycled police sketch of a large-eyed man in a black hood, a 4chan image from 2010 ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/creepy-hooded-guy-an-intruder)). Critics call the imagery basic Photoshop under a VHS filter, but grant that the creatures are the subgenre's most influential ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2198998/best-analog-horror-series-ranked/)). **Status:** the creator has faced grooming allegations since March 2024 ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/what-were-the-fetish-and-grooming-allegations-against-the-mandela-catalogue-creator-alex-kister-mandela-catalogue-leaks-and-controversy-explained)). **Licence:** owned (film rights sold, [Fangoria](https://www.fangoria.com/?p=116688)). Do not reference it.

**15. The Walten Files (Martin Walls).**
Possessed animatronics framed as a training tape. It began as an offshoot of *Five Nights at Freddy's*, and critics praise its grief and trauma themes ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2198998/best-analog-horror-series-ranked/)). **Licence:** Walls's.

**16. Kane Pixels' *Backrooms* (tone only).**
Blender scenes with a simulated camcorder and VHS noise ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Parsons)). Slashfilm credits him with keeping it simple while imitators added monsters, blood and lore ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2198998/best-analog-horror-series-ranked/)). Parsons says liminal photos appeal because they "feel authorless", like a dream of your childhood home "extrapolated" ([Interview Magazine, 2026](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/kane-parsons-backrooms-a24)). **Licence:** Parsons and A24. Our Backrooms must not borrow his entities or shots.

**On saturation:** critics describe analog horror as oversaturated ([Dread Central](https://dreadcentral.com/editorials/439064/analog-horror-and-its-blueprints-for-horrors-future)). A "How to Make an Analog Horror" meme mocks "spooky faces, random jump scares, and the same VHS effect" ([Slasher, 2023](https://www.slasher.pub/p/the-shocking-decline-of-analog-horror)). Rowan Long warns that "it's easy to make this sort of thing cheesy and not scary". He says the craft is "hiding the strange within the image", and lists the fans' tired tropes, including "shadowed face + realistic eyes" and unexplained text-to-speech voices ([Long 2023](https://info.umkc.edu/sosland_journal/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Long_AnalysisOfAnalogHorror.pdf)).

### D. "Witnessed" figures: sleep paralysis, cryptids, contactees

**17. The Hat Man, shadow people and the "tall man".**
Dark silhouettes with human profiles at the edge of vision. The Hat Man wears a brimmed hat, has few or no features, and witnesses "feel him staring". He is linked to sleep paralysis ([Wikipedia, Shadow person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_person)). A typical account: a tall, faceless man in a trenchcoat and wide hat stands in the corner, never speaks and never comes closer ([USC Folklore Archive](https://folklore.usc.edu/sleep-paralysis-and-the-hat-man/)). Cheyne et al. (1999) group sleep-paralysis hallucinations into "Intruder" (sensed presence, fear, visions), "Incubus" (pressure on the chest) and bodily factors, and note that the descriptions are consistent across cultures ([PubMed 10487786](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10487786/)). **Why it works:** a real neurological experience gives the figure a stable shape: tall, dark, clothed, still, watching. **Licence:** free.

**18. The Grinning Man / Indrid Cold (1966).**
A contactee's "gently smiling" visitor and two boys' "strangest guy we ever saw": very tall, in shiny green coveralls, with a wide grin and beady eyes, standing behind a fence in a place he could not have reached. John Keel merged the two ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indrid_Cold)). **Lesson:** ordinary clothes on a figure standing somewhere impossible. **Licence:** the reports are free. Keel's books are copyrighted.

**19. The Fresno Nightcrawler (2007).**
A pair of white "walking pants" on a security camera, with a second, smaller one following ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresno_nightcrawler)). Skeptoid's verdict is that it is more cute than scary ([Skeptoid](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/4826)). **Lesson:** a wrong body plan without menace, stillness or context reads as comic. Strangeness alone is not enough.

**20. Skinwalker and "mimic" lore.**
The internet version is a tall, pale humanoid that mimics voices. In Diné belief a skinwalker is a human witch, and many Native voices call the pop-culture use appropriation. Adrienne Keene said of Rowling's use: "you can't just claim and take a living tradition" ([Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/new-harry-potter-stories-enrage-native-americans); [overview, low quality](https://www.urbanlegendsmysteryandmyth.com/2025/09/skinwalkers-in-pop-culture-from-navajo.html)). **Recommendation:** do not use it. Voice mimicry as a general motif appears elsewhere (Hachishakusama).

### E. Japanese urban legends

**21. Kuchisake-onna (the slit-mouthed woman).**
A tall woman in a surgical mask with long black hair, carrying scissors. She asks "Am I pretty?" and then shows a mouth slit from ear to ear. **Escapes:** answer "average", say you are late, throw *bekko ame* candy or money for her to pick up, or say "pomade" three times. In 1979 the legend spread nationally and children were escorted home ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuchisake-onna)). **Licence:** folk, free. The distraction motif is directly useful.

**22. Teke Teke.**
The upper half of a girl dragging herself on her elbows, named for the scraping sound she makes. She cuts victims in half at the waist ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_Teke)). **Lesson:** a scraping sound heard well before she is seen, and a body at floor height.

**23. Hanako-san.**
A girl with a bob haircut and a red skirt in the third stall of a third-floor school toilet. You knock three times and ask if she is there. Folklorist Matthew Meyer dates the legend to the 1950s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanako-san)). **Lesson:** an exact address (floor, stall, knocks) makes a ritual, which suits numbered floors and cells.

**24. Hachishakusama ("Eight-Feet", 2ch, 2008).**
A woman about 2.4 m tall in a pale dress with a hat. She makes a humanlike "po… po…" sound that never becomes words. She taps on windows and imitates the grandfather's voice at the door, and the boy knows the voice is false because his grandfather promised not to call him before morning. **Protection:** one night in a sealed room with talismans and salt in the corners, and Jizō statues at the village edges ([yokai.jp](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/hachishakusama); [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/8-foot-tall-woman-japan/)). **Imaging:** her head shows above a two-metre hedge, so an everyday object measures her. **Licence:** an anonymous but authored modern post. Use the motifs only.

**25. Kunekune (about 2000–2003).**
A slender white human shape far off across rice fields, whose limbs wriggle even when there is no wind, seen around noon on hot summer days ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunekune_(urban_legend))). It is harmless at a distance, but "attempting to understand its true identity using binoculars" causes insanity ([yokai.jp](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/kunekune), via search). **Why it works:** a rule about how closely you look, and simple form with wrong motion. **Licence:** an authored post. Use the motifs only.

### F. Slavic folklore (public domain)

**26. Domovoi.**
An old grey-haired man with "flashing eyes", or the shape of the householder, a dead ancestor or an animal. He guards the house, warns of deaths, and turns hostile if the household behaves badly. Families leave him offerings and invite him along when they move ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domovoy)). The domovoi is one East Slavic reading of sleep paralysis, according to the title of a 2018 paper by Lazareva, *The brownie, the walking dead and the soul that left the body*. I saw only the title, via [Shagi/Steps](https://shagi.ranepa.ru/en/steps/article?viewid=815). **Lesson:** a house spirit whose mood follows how you treat the place.

**27. Kikimora.**
A small, ugly, hunchbacked, thin old woman, her head "no larger than a thimble" (Lyadov). Bilibin's 1934 drawing gives her a chicken-like form. She spins at night. When the house is in order she helps. When it isn't, she whistles, breaks dishes and makes noises at night ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikimora)). **Lesson:** wrong in size, heard more than seen, and its behaviour depends on order.

**28. Likho (Likho One-Eyed).**
A one-eyed being, often a thin old woman in black. It cheats people and "rides on their necks", driving them to drown. The proverb is "Не буди лихо, пока оно тихо": don't wake Likho while it is quiet ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likho)). **Lesson:** a sleeping danger with a rule about noise, and a single eye that reads as wrong at once.

**29. Poludnitsa (Lady Midday).**
A woman in white, sometimes a girl or an old hag, with a scythe or shears, sometimes a whirl of dust. She appears at the hottest hour in the fields and asks hard questions. Those who fail to answer, or change the subject, are struck down. She personifies sunstroke, and workers avoided her by pausing at the midday Angelus ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poludnica)). **Lesson:** tied to a time and a light, and defeated by stopping work: an ideal learnable rule.

**30. Upyr.**
An East Slavic revenant, with "wolf-like" glowing eyes in some accounts, that causes suffocation and sleepwalking. Signs were a corpse with a flushed face and no rigor mortis. Countermeasures included scattering poppy seed or knotted threads that it must pick up or untangle, which keeps it busy ([Wikipedia, Upyr/Upiór](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upyr)). **Lesson:** a compulsion the player can exploit.

**31. Babay / Babayka.**
A night bogeyman who is rarely described, "so children can imagine him in whatever form frightens them most". Sometimes he is a pitch-black, crooked old man with a sack and a cane. He takes children who stay awake, watches through windows and makes rustling and knocking sounds if they are awake, or hides under the bed. The name probably comes from Tatar for "old man" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babay_(Slavic_folklore))).

### G. Russian and Soviet urban and internet horror beyond samosbor

**32. Soviet children's horror stories (*страшилки*).**
The structure is warning, then violation, then punishment, and things happen when the child is alone without parents ([mel.fm, citing Loiter](https://mel.fm/blog/anna-sivakova/94712-devochka-grob-na-kolesikakh-uzhe-edet-po-tvoyey-ulitse-pochemu-deti-tak-lyubyat-strashilki)). In the **Coffin on Wheels**, a girl breaks her dead mother's ban. Then the radio, the TV and the phone each repeat that a coffin on wheels is coming to her home, and the story ends at the door and the peephole. In the **Black/Yellow Curtains**, curtains bought against a grandmother's warning strangle the family or coax children out of the window. In the **Black Tram**, a hearse-like tram's route number is the boy's birth year ([New East Digital Archive, 2017](https://www.new-east-archive.org/articles/show/9177/soviet-scares-creep-yourself-out-halloween-with-these-ussr-urban-legends)). Eduard Uspensky, who collected these stories from children's letters, read the killer coffins and buses as echoes of the NKVD's "black ravens", the vans used for night arrests ([mel.fm](https://mel.fm/blog/anna-sivakova/94712-devochka-grob-na-kolesikakh-uzhe-edet-po-tvoyey-ulitse-pochemu-deti-tak-lyubyat-strashilki)). The **Queen of Spades** summoning: at midnight you draw a stair of 13 steps and a door on a mirror in lipstick, and a face appears when the candle crackles ([mel.fm](https://mel.fm/vospitaniye/4189562-seance)). **Licence:** folk, free. Uspensky's books are copyrighted.

**33. The Elevator Game.**
Ride alone in a building of 10 or more floors and press a set floor sequence (4-2-6-2-10-5, then 1). A woman may get in at 5, and you must not speak to her or look at her. If the lift goes up instead of down, you have arrived in another world ([Meta store listing, describing the ritual](https://www.meta.com/experiences/pcvr/the-elevator-ritual/885195431603573/); [Skeptophilia](https://www.skeptophilia.com/2023/05/going-up.html)). Its origin is undocumented. **Licence:** anonymous, so the motif is free. It fits the Building's lifts.

**34. Others.** Runet's best-known "deadly files" include *БЕЗНОГNМ*, a "cursed" rabbit video from 2008 ([T-Zh](https://t-j.ru/list/creepypastas-to-read-in-the-dark/)). I could not verify any "Бледный человек".

### H. The "moves only when unseen" lineage (for the Watcher)

SCP-173 (4chan, June 2007) is a concrete statue that moves only when no one is looking directly at it. Staff keep eye contact and announce their blinks, and unobserved it makes a scraping sound. Its original image was an Izumi Katō sculpture, used without permission and removed in 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCP-173)). The Weeping Angels (*Doctor Who*, "Blink", 2007) came from a Dorset graveyard statue and the children's game Grandmother's Footsteps. They were played by actresses and shown as freeze frames ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeping_Angel)). **Licence:** SCP is CC BY-SA and needs attribution. The Angels belong to the BBC. The playground-game motif is free.

## Part 2 — How entities are pictured so that they frighten

**Theory in four lines.**
- **Mori (1970):** affinity drops as a figure approaches human likeness without reaching it, movement deepens the drop, and a corpse sits in the valley ([IEEE Spectrum translation by MacDorman & Kageki, 2012](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-uncanny-valley)).
- **MacDorman et al. (2009):** atypical facial proportions disturb more on photorealistic faces than on stylised ones, and a mismatch between eye size or texture and the rest of the face is especially eerie ([*Computers in Human Behavior* 25(3)](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/e8fb6458-ddad-4067-b877-1207d720ba0c)). Mori advised robot designers to stay at the first peak. Horror wants the valley floor: near-human figures with one specific error against a realistic rest.
- **McAndrew & Koehnke (2016):** "creepiness" is a response to *uncertainty* about whether a threat exists. A clearly visible threat produces fear, not creepiness ([*New Ideas in Psychology* 43](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/McAndrew-Koehnke-2016.pdf)).
- **Carroll (1990), *The Philosophy of Horror*:** monsters are threatening and *impure*, meaning they violate categories (after Mary Douglas). He names five building methods ([Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/products/9780203361894); [summary](https://new.express.adobe.com/page/PLDOg/)):
  - *fusion*: categories in one body, such as living and dead;
  - *fission*: one body split across times or doubles, such as the werewolf or the double;
  - *magnification*: an ordinary thing made huge;
  - *massification*: an ordinary thing in a swarm;
  - *horrific metonymy*: impurity carried by what surrounds the monster. One secondary summary instead glosses it as figures that look normal but feel wrong, so check the book before relying on either gloss.

**Fisher (2016)** adds the *eerie*: "something present where there should be nothing", or the reverse ([The Quietus extract](https://thequietus.com/?p=21524)). That is our pillar 1.

**1. Found photo and VHS degradation.** It works because the brain believes the format. Henderson says found footage makes it "easier for your brain to believe" ([Daily Dead](https://dailydead.com/qa-trevor-henderson-discusses-the-found-footage-style-of-his-fascinating-horror-artwork/)). It fails when it is the whole idea, as with a filter over a face ([Slasher](https://www.slasher.pub/p/the-shocking-decline-of-analog-horror)). The first-person equivalents are low light, noise, flashlight falloff, and small degraded views, not a VHS overlay.

**2. Scale and distance.** Slender Man is a figure in the background that nobody in the photo notices ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender_Man)). Hachishakusama's height is shown by a hedge and a car window ([yokai.jp](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/hachishakusama)). Kunekune is only ever seen far away ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunekune_(urban_legend))), and its danger lies in looking closer. Carroll's *magnification* is the giant. Henderson's subtler lesson is "slightly off proportions" ([Daily Dead](https://dailydead.com/qa-trevor-henderson-discusses-the-found-footage-style-of-his-fascinating-horror-artwork/)). Something 15% too tall next to a door frame is worse than 40 feet in an empty field.

**3. Partial visibility.** *Skinamarink*'s director keeps characters barely in frame and says "Scary fades, creepy is forever" ([Jezebel](https://jezebel.com/skinamarink-horror-movie-interview-1849982410)). Withholding converts fear into McAndrew's productive uncertainty.

**4. Wrong proportions: too tall, too thin, limbs, neck.** Botet's real long limbs read as uncanny *because* they are real ([Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/how-javier-botets-rare-marfan-syndrome-enabled-his-acting-career-1022027/)). MacDorman's result predicts that proportion errors hit harder on realistic surfaces ([2009](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/e8fb6458-ddad-4067-b877-1207d720ba0c)). Since our Building surfaces are now real photographs, wrong proportions placed in them will be strong. Long Horse's neck and the Crooked Man's forward neck and tilted head are the classic neck errors ([Wikipedia, Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Henderson); [Ghost in My Machine](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2021/08/11/encyclopaedia-of-the-impossible-the-crooked-man/)).

**5. Faces: missing, covered, smiling, too wide.**
- **Missing:** Slender Man; Henderson's eyeless creatures, whose intentions become "completely ambiguous" ([Reactor](https://reactormag.com/artist-trevor-henderson-siren-head-interview/)); the Hat Man.
- **Covered:** Kuchisake-onna's mask, which turns the reveal into an event.
- **Turned away:** the Rake sitting with its back to the sleepers; Local 58's "Woman in Profile".
- **Too-wide smiles:** Jeff, Smile Dog, Widemouth, the Grinning Man. These are the faces that turned comic. A grin is a complete, readable expression, and a readable face lowers uncertainty (McAndrew), so it becomes a mascot.
- **Recommendation:** hide or cover the face, or show a face that is *almost* normal. Never show a fully lit grinning face.

**6. Eyes and eyeshine.** Humans have no tapetum lucidum, so a flashlight on a human face gives no eyeshine. Animal eyeshine is green, gold, white with a blue rim, or red, and its colour shifts with viewing angle ([Wikipedia, Tapetum lucidum](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Eyeshine)). Eyeshine in a human-shaped head is therefore a precise *fusion* signal (person plus animal) that needs no gore. Folklore gives "flashing eyes" to the domovoi and "wolf-like" eyes to the upyr ([Domovoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domovoy); [Upyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upyr)). Likho has one eye. A *single* eyeshine point, or two points spaced wrongly, reads wrong at once. Avoid "shadowed face + realistic eyes" ([Long 2023](https://info.umkc.edu/sosland_journal/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Long_AnalysisOfAnalogHorror.pdf)), meaning eyes that look like photographs floating in black.

**7. Stillness against jerky motion; "doesn't move while seen".** Mori says motion deepens the valley ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-uncanny-valley)). Sadako's walk was performed backwards by a kabuki-trained actress and played in reverse ([Wikipedia, Rie Inō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rie_In%C5%8D)). The Weeping Angels were shown as freeze frames ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeping_Angel)). The "moves when unseen" rule (SCP-173, the Angels, Grandmother's Footsteps) turns blinking and turning away into a risk. In the short *Lights Out* (2013), the monster "only exists when the lights are turned out" ([Wikipedia, Sandberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_F._Sandberg)), so every switch-flip is a cut. Two motion vocabularies work: **total stillness, then a change found after a cut**, or **wrong mechanics** (reversed, stepped, joints out of order). Smooth, ordinary locomotion is the weakest choice.

**8. Posture.** The Rake crouches, the Crooked Man leans his neck forward and tilts his head, the Hat Man stands in the corner, Hachishakusama bends to look in at the window. A stoop under a ceiling also tells you the height. A tilted head reads as attention and curiosity, which is worse than aggression.

**9. Human clothes on wrong bodies.** Slender Man's suit, the Grinning Man's coveralls, the Hat Man's hat and coat, Mr. Bear's costume. Clothes say "person" and the body contradicts them: Carroll's impurity at its cheapest. For the Building: a housecoat, a quilted *vatnik*, a headscarf, a work smock, kept documentary rather than comic.

**10. Sheets and cloth.** The Soviet "Black Sheet" is a children's monster ([AST/Uspensky](https://ast.ru/book/krasnaya-ruka-chyernaya-prostynya-zelyenye-paltsy-883117/)). Curtains that strangle ([New East Archive](https://www.new-east-archive.org/articles/show/9177/soviet-scares-creep-yourself-out-halloween-with-these-ussr-urban-legends)) are cloth as agent. Cloth hides the body plan while hinting at it: a sheet over something too tall, or a dust sheet whose folds fall wrong because the body underneath is wrong.

**11. Darkness and silhouettes.** Shadow people are pure silhouettes seen at the edge of vision ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_person)). In *Amnesia: The Dark Descent* (2010), "sanity is reduced by staying in the dark for too long, witnessing unsettling events or looking directly at monsters", so hiding in the dark and looking both cost something ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesia:_The_Dark_Descent)). A silhouette is known by its outline, so the outline must be distinctive.

## Part 3 — Implications for our creatures

Engine facts I assume: 74° vertical FOV; at 16:9 that gives about 107° horizontal. Eye height about 1.6 m. Light comes from fluorescent fixtures and lamps placed in the world, plus the player's flashlight. Creatures are skinned Blender meshes with vertex colours and morphs, with emissive maps, alpha and fog.

### Imaging principles

**P1. Let the architecture measure the creature, not the frame.** With a 74° vertical FOV, the top of the frame at distance *d* sits at about 1.6 m + 0.75·*d*. A 2.4 m figure therefore stays fully in frame until about 1 m away, and a 3 m figure until about 1.9 m. Cropping won't sell the height, so show it against door lintels, light fittings, and a stoop under a low ceiling (Hachishakusama's hedge). Keep errors around 10–25% ("slightly off", Henderson). A 2.3 m figure that has to duck under a Soviet door is worse than a 4 m giant.

**P2. At distance, a creature is an outline and (sometimes) two points.** At 20 m a 2.4 m figure is about 9% of screen height, roughly 100 px at 1080p. At 40 m it is about 50 px. Spend the vertex budget on the outline (neck angle, shoulder line, headwear, how the limbs hang), so each creature can be identified as a black cut-out.

**P3. Eyeshine is a retroreflection, not a glow.**
- **Physics:** it should appear only when the *player's flashlight* hits the eyes *and* the creature faces the player. Under overhead fluorescents the eyes stay dull. That is physically right, and it lets the flashlight "find" eyes.
- **Implementation:** drive an emissive eye region with a per-instance value (flashlight irradiance at the head × facing) rather than a new texture lookup.
- **Colour and count:** use colour per species (green, gold, white with a blue rim). One point for one creature, wrong spacing for another.
- **For the Smiler redesign:** drop the always-visible eyes and the full grin. Show eyeshine only in the beam, and only part of a mouth.

**P4. Two motion vocabularies, never ordinary walking.**
- **(a) Statue:** nothing moves while it is in view or in the beam. Changes happen during the player's cuts, such as turning away, a light flicker, or a door closing.
- **(b) Wrong mechanics:** stepped keyframes held for 3–6 frames, then a snap; gaits authored and played in reverse (the Sadako method); joints that move in the wrong order (head first, then the body follows).
- **Morph targets:** use them for slow neck lengthening or a head tilt that happens *only while the creature is unseen*.

**P5. Hide or cover the face, and keep one error specific.** A face may be covered (sheet, mask, wet hair, headscarf pulled forward), turned away, in profile, missing, or almost normal with one wrong element: a single eye, eyeshine in a human face, or a mouth set too low. Never show a fully lit grin (the Jeff and Smile Dog lesson). Never pair photo-real eyes with a black face (the "shadowed face + realistic eyes" cliché).

**P6. Realistic surface, wrong structure.** Following MacDorman, photo-sourced rooms and grounded vertex-colour skin (dirt, sweat, worn cloth) make proportion errors land. Avoid stylised gore and Henderson's "sharp teeth, angry brows, claws".

**P7. Small degraded views are our "found photo".** Use peepholes (fisheye, dim landing light), wired-glass panes, gaps under doors, a gallery across the foggy void, or a CCTV monitor. Each limits what the player sees, so use them for reveals instead of a global VHS filter.

**P8. Each creature gets a sound signature, heard first.** Siren Head's mimicry, Hachishakusama's "po… po…", Teke Teke's scraping and SCP-173's concrete scrape are all heard before they are seen. Give each creature a unique sound: a non-word human sound, a mechanical scrape, a spinning wheel, a radio. Never use a voice reading lore. Pillar 3 also allows the reverse: *silence* as the signature (see Likho).

**P9. Rules come from folklore structures, delivered in the world.** Folklore has already made these forms learnable: a question with a right answer (Poludnitsa), a compulsion to exploit (upyr seeds, candy), a time or light window (noon), a protecting room (Hachishakusama), a warning-violation-countdown (Coffin on Wheels), a trusted voice with a tell. Deliver rules through M.'s notes, cleaner logs and posted Soviet instructions that turn wrong (the Local 58 method), never through tutorials.

**P10. Keep Soviet details documentary.** Because "Soviet creepypasta" is an established parody genre ([Mirvoda 2018](https://press.psu.ru/index.php/philology/article/download/1633/1204/3523)), avoid clichés of Soviet life used as costume. Use real objects: the wired radio receiver in each flat (*радиоточка*), enamel signs, padded doors, housecoats, quilted jackets. Do not dress them up.

### Creature concepts (public-domain folklore, or general motifs only)

**C1. The Noon Woman (from Poludnitsa).** *Backrooms, the Lobby and offices.*
**Look:** a tall woman in a washed-out work smock and headscarf, holding something long and thin. She is seen only in overexposed light, her face lost in the glare.
**Event:** at "noon" (a place-based event per zone: the office's light map surges white and the hum rises), she stands at the far end of the room.
**Rule the player learns:** *when the lights go white, stop working.* Running, using items, or switching on the flashlight during the surge make her close in, one step per rising tone. Standing still until the lights dim makes her leave. If she is already close, she "asks" (three rising tones), and the player must answer each one with **cry out**. That keeps her back, but Hounds can hear the cries.
**Taught by:** M.'s note ("At noon the workers stopped. I stopped.") and an office poster with lunch hours. **Damage:** heatstroke-style blur and stamina drain.
**Source:** Lady Midday, who struck those who didn't answer and was avoided by pausing at the midday Angelus ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poludnica)). Public domain.

**C2. Likho.** *The Building, dead factory floors.*
**Look:** a very thin, very tall figure in black, folded and asleep on a machine bed or in a corner, under a tarp. One eyeshine point in the beam.
**Signature:** silence. Likho lives where the hum stops (pillar 3).
**Behaviour:** it hears but cannot see. Noise (running, dropped items, cry out) raises a wake meter. Awake, it does not chase. It drops onto the player and "rides the neck": the camera stoops, running and stamina recovery stop, and breathing sounds close.
**Rule:** *walk where it's quiet.* If it has woken, shed it by passing through a hermetic door (it cannot fit past the sill).
**Proverb:** "Не буди лихо, пока оно тихо" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likho)). Public domain. The door-sill escape is our invention, not folklore.

**C3. The Counter (from upyr seeds and Kuchisake-onna's candy).** *Backrooms, utility halls.*
**Look:** a crouched person in a soaked undershirt, flushed in the vertex colour (the upyr's "flushed face"), with fingers too long. It mutters numbers.
**Behaviour:** it is fast and hunts by sight at medium range.
**Compulsion:** it must stop to pick up and unfold every piece of paper on the floor.
**Rule:** *drop paper behind you to buy time.* The cost is that it also collects your trail markers, so marked paths through its halls vanish.
**Taught by:** M.'s note ("It reads everything. I stopped marking here.").
**Sources:** poppy seed and knots that keep the upiór busy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upyr)); candy thrown for the slit-mouthed woman ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuchisake-onna)). Both public domain.

**C4. The One Who Knocks (Babay, with a trusted-voice motif).** *The Building, shelter cells at night and during samosbor.*
**Look:** never seen in the open. Through the fisheye peephole: an empty landing; or a crooked, sack-shaped figure much too close; or only the top of a head, because it is too tall for the peephole's view.
**Behaviour:** like Babay, it comes for whoever is awake. While the player moves or keeps the lamp on, knocking and rustling build at the door and the vent. Staying dark and still makes it pass. It also speaks the all-clear in a resident's voice.
**Rule:** *never open for a voice; open only for the siren's all-clear tone.* The tell, after Hachishakusama's grandfather who promised not to call before morning, is that the cleaners' log says they never speak at doors.
**Sources:** Babay ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babay_(Slavic_folklore))), public domain. The voice with a tell is a general motif, not the 2ch text. It fits the canon "something knocks back".

**C5. The Spinner (Kikimora).** *Backrooms, habitable zone and M.'s camps.*
**Look:** a thimble-sized head on a thin, hunched body, with bird-like legs and a shawl. Seen small and high up, on top of a wardrobe or along a cable tray. Mostly heard as a spinning-wheel whirr and thread being pulled.
**Behaviour:** if a camp is left in disorder, it breaks things at night and *re-folds and turns paper markers*, yours and M.'s, to lead you astray. Leave the camp tidy and it stays quiet, and may even put back something you dropped.
**Rule:** *keep camps in order; trust only markers folded the way M. folds them.* M.'s notes show her fold, and the kikimora's folds are mirror images.
**Source:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikimora). Public domain.

**C6 (optional, a system rather than a body). The Radio Countdown (Coffin on Wheels).** *The Building.*
**Trigger:** taking an item from a door marked in red.
**Behaviour:** the wired radio in each cell begins announcing where "it" is: block, floor, gallery, door.
**Rule:** *put it back before the radio names your door.* If you don't, there is a knock (C4).
**Source:** the warning–violation–countdown structure of Soviet children's horror ([New East Archive](https://www.new-east-archive.org/articles/show/9177/soviet-scares-creep-yourself-out-halloween-with-these-ussr-urban-legends); [mel.fm](https://mel.fm/blog/anna-sivakova/94712-devochka-grob-na-kolesikakh-uzhe-edet-po-tvoyey-ulitse-pochemu-deti-tak-lyubyat-strashilki)). Public domain.
