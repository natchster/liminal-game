# Decayed mid-century underground facilities

Research for the deeper floors' remake ([`../plan/FACILITY.md`](../plan/FACILITY.md)), compiled 2026-11-17 by a research agent and kept as found. Scope: real underground sites built roughly 1940-1980 that still stand or are documented in decay. Nothing here comes from any game: the mood the brief names (a game's underground facility) is reached only through real places.

**Conventions**
- Every fact carries its source URL in brackets. Full list at the end.
- **[unverified]**: I could not confirm it from a page I opened. **[unverified: excerpt]** means I saw it only in a search-engine excerpt of the cited page and could not open the page itself (403, scanned PDF, etc.).
- "≈" marks a unit conversion I did myself. Sources give the other numbers.
- Where sources disagree, both figures are given.

## 3. Materials and how they age

**Poured reinforced concrete**
- Diefenbunker: 32,000 t of concrete, 5,000 t of steel [W-Dief], rebar up to 6.5 cm [Legion].
- Greenbrier walls are 2 ft [W-Greek]. Titan II walls run up to 8 ft [W-TMM]. SLAC housing walls are 1.5 ft [SLAC-62].
- Säve: a reinforced-concrete structure built inside a granite chamber [SubBrit-Save].
- Titan II LCC: a reinforced-concrete structure grounded by "a 1/4-inch steel shell", containing a "three-level, steel, shock-isolation cage". The cableways have steel floors [TO-21M].

**Shotcrete (gunite)**
- Invented by Carl Akeley and first used in 1907, patented in 1911 [W-Shot].
- Dry-mix was standard until wet-mix came in during the 1950s. "Shotcrete" became the official generic name by 1951 [W-Shot].
- Used to line mine, subway and road tunnels, usually reinforced with mesh, rods or fibres [W-Shot].
- Cheyenne Mountain's support combined reinforced concrete and gunite linings, rock bolting, chain-link fabric, and epoxy and cement grouting [unverified: excerpt] [IC8294].

**Rock bolts and mesh**
- Systematic use began in US mines in the 1920s. Civil tunnels in the US and Australia adopted them in the late 1940s. Snowy Mountains engineers tried 4 m expanding-anchor bolts in 1947 [W-Bolt].
- Always installed in a pattern. They can hold wire or chain-link mesh [W-Bolt].
- Cheyenne Mountain has 110,000 bolts, 6-36 ft long [R50].

**Corrugated steel liner plate**
- First steel liner plates were installed in 1926 in the Moffat Tunnel [unverified: excerpt] [Armtec].
- Bolted 4-flange plates, 16 in and 24 in wide [unverified: excerpt] [Jennmar].
- The Diefenbunker blast tunnel is a corrugated-steel arch with a flat concrete floor and concrete curbs, as seen in the Commons photo in §6 [C-Dief-BT]. This is my observation from the photo.

**Painted steel and grating**
- In the Titan II corridor photo: steel bar-grating floor panels at doorways, with yellow-and-black striped edges, and pale-green-painted steel framing in the cableway [C-TMM-LH]. This is my observation from the photo.

**Tiles**
- I found no source for glazed tile in these specific facilities. [unverified]

**Decay**
- *Efflorescence.* Salt dissolved in the material is carried out by water and left on the surface as it evaporates. Primary efflorescence is "a white, fluffy deposit, that can normally be brushed off". Secondary efflorescence is driven by outside chlorides [W-Effl].
- *Calcite straws (calthemite).* Hyperalkaline seepage (pH about 9-14) from concrete takes up CO₂ and deposits calcite [W-Calth].
  - Straw stalactites are thin-walled and hollow and can grow up to 2 mm per day, about 200 times faster than cave speleothems. Slower drips make wider straws [W-Calth].
  - Drips faster than one per minute build floor stalagmites and flowstone. Drips 25-30 min apart can seal the straw tip [W-Calth].
  - They also form rimstone (gours) and "popcorn" [W-Calth].
  - Usually white. Red, orange or yellow comes from rusting rebar, and green or blue from copper [W-Calth].
- *Rust bloom, staining and spalling.* Corrosion products take more volume than steel and crack and spall the concrete cover. Rust washes out of cracks as brown stains [Rimkus] [unverified: excerpt]. Vendors claim 2-6× expansion [HDG] [unverified: excerpt].
- *Flooding*
  - Soudan flooded on 18 June 2024; pumps, electrics and trains were damaged [W-Soudan].
  - Mittelwerk is partly flooded [SubBrit-MW]. The Commons photo shows standing water over rusted rails [C-MW01].
  - Decommissioned Atlas silos often filled with groundwater and rainwater, and some can be dived [unverified: excerpt] [ScoutNY].
  - The Diefenbunker's first site was abandoned over groundwater [W-Dief].
- *Stripping and abandonment.* Atlas F silos were salvaged down to structural walls [MB-AtlasF]. The Diefenbunker was stripped clean after it closed [Legion].
- *Climate underground*
  - Cheyenne rock is 50-55 °F, with buildings kept at 70-72 °F [R50].
  - Michigan salt mine: 56-60 °F [MSU-Salt].
  - Diefenbunker: about 17 °C [Legion].
  - The Cruachan tunnel is warm and humid enough for tropical plants [W-Cru].

**Fixtures of the era**
- *RLM dome reflector.* Traced to a 1912 Benjamin Electric design, with a standard set in 1919. Usually porcelain enamel [unverified: excerpt] [Lamptech], [Bock].
- *Vapour-proof jar fixtures.* A threaded glass globe in a cast-aluminium cage, by Appleton or Crouse-Hinds, in the 1910s-1940s. Known only from dealer descriptions [unverified: excerpt] [UrbanRemains].

**How these places were lit**
- Diefenbunker: fluorescent tubes, loud enough that staff switched them off [Legion].
- In the Diefenbunker blast-tunnel photo, bare lamps hang singly from the crown at regular intervals [C-Dief-BT].
- In the Titan II corridor photo, ceiling fluorescent fixtures run along the cableway [C-TMM-LH]. The T.O. lists a lighting panel on silo level 6 and one in the control centre [TO-21M].
- In the Manapouri photo, rows of lamps hang from a dark ceiling above the generator floor [C-Mana].
- The three photo notes above are my observations, not measured spacing.
- Minimum light levels in codes:
  - US construction code: 5 foot-candles (≈54 lux) for "tunnels, shafts, and general underground work areas", 10 fc at headings during drilling and mucking, 5 fc for corridors. It refers to ANSI A11.1-1965 *Practice for Industrial Lighting* for other cases [1926.56].
  - US coal mines: at least 0.06 foot-lamberts of surface brightness in working places, at most 120 fL [unverified: excerpt] [30CFR75.1719].
- Road tunnels: CIE Publication 26 (1973) set entrance-zone lighting requirements that proved hard to meet. It was replaced by CIE 61 (1984) and CIE 88 (1990) [unverified: excerpt] [SWOV].
- I found no primary source giving fixture spacing or mounting height in these facilities. [unverified]

## 5. Colour and paint

**Safety colour code history**
- Faber Birren and DuPont created an industrial colour safety code during WWII. The National Safety Council approved it in 1944 [unverified: excerpt] [BoingBoing].
- The Z53 line began as an American War Standard in 1945 [unverified: excerpt] [ANSI-blog].
- J.J. Keller instead says ASA work began in 1946 and the code was approved in 1953 [unverified: excerpt] [JJK-cc]. The two accounts conflict.
- OSHA cites USAS/ANSI Z53.1-1967 [JJK-Z53]. The 1979 edition was superseded by Z535 [NISTIR].

**Hazard stripes**
- The older OSHA text names "alternate stripes of black and white" and "black and white checkers" for traffic [JJK-cc].
- Yellow-and-black striped floor edges appear in the Titan II corridor photo [C-TMM-LH].

**Pipe colour codes**
- *ANSI/ASME A13.1.* First affirmed 1928, reaffirmed 1945, revised 1956, 1975 and 1981 [unverified: excerpt] [GS-A13]. The 1996 scheme [W-Pipe]:
  - yellow with black text: hazardous materials;
  - green with white: non-hazardous liquids;
  - blue with white: non-hazardous gases;
  - red with white: firefighting.
- *UK BS 1710 basic colours*, with BS 4800 references, from the 2019 edition [W-Pipe]:
  - water: green 12 D 45;
  - steam: silver-grey 10 A 03;
  - oils: brown 06 C 39;
  - gases: yellow ochre 08 C 35;
  - acids and alkalis: violet 22 C 37;
  - air: light blue 20 E 51;
  - other liquids: black 00 E 53;
  - electrical and ventilation: orange 06 E 51.
- *BS 1710 safety colours* [W-Pipe]: fire red 04 E 53, warning yellow 08 E 51, fresh water auxiliary blue 18 E 53.
- Earlier editions of BS 1710 [unverified].

**Two-tone dados and institutional schemes**
- No standard or conservation report found.
- Photographic evidence: the Titan II corridor has a dark (near-black) lower wall to about hip height with pale green above, plus yellow/black floor-edge stripes [C-TMM-LH]. Observation only.
- "Hospital green" is credited to the surgeon Harry Sherman around WWI. This rests on a single blog-type source [unverified: excerpt] [MedCity].
- BS 381C 216 "Eau de Nil" and 361 "Light Stone" are standard British colour names. Their use in bunkers is [unverified] [e-paint].
- A visitor blog reports deep blue, mustard yellow and light blue walls at the York Cold War Bunker [unverified: excerpt] [TimeTourist].
